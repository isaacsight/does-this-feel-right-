One of the most forceful stories he told on behalf of the utility of philosophy Was what has become known as "The Allegory of the Cave". It is perhaps the most famous allegory in philosophy. This was a story that was intended, as he wrote, to compare "[t]he effect of education and the lack of it on our nature." At the start of Book 7 of his masterpiece, "The Republic", Plato tells us about some people living imprisoned in a cave They've always lived there and don't know anything of the outside world.
