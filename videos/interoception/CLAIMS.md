## Claims the film makes

| # | Claim in the film | Source | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Interoception is a real sensory channel — the body continuously reports on its own internal state | Craig (2002), *Nat Rev Neurosci* 3:655–666, "How do you feel?" | **High** | Uncontroversial. Insula as hub: Critchley et al. (2004), *Nat Neurosci* 7:189–195 |
| 2 | The same arousal level accompanies very different emotions | See #6 | **High** | Stated in the film as "same number, three feelings" — carried by #6, not asserted alone |
| 3 | Most people are poor at counting their own heartbeats | Heartbeat-counting task, Schandry (1981), *Psychophysiology* 18:483–488 | **Medium-high** | The *finding* is robust; the *task* is contested — see #4 |
| 4 | Some people are guessing from what they think their heart should be doing | Critiques of the counting task: Ring & Brener; Zamariola et al. (2018), *Biol Psychol* | **Medium-high** | This is a criticism OF the standard measure. The film uses it honestly — as evidence the signal is thin, not as a hard result |
| 5 | 1962: adrenaline plus a cheerful room produced cheerfulness; an angry room, anger | Schachter & Singer (1962), *J Abnorm Soc Psychol* 69:379–399 | **High** *that the study exists and reported this* | The film explicitly does not endorse the result |
| 6 | It has not replicated well | Marshall & Zimbardo (1979) and Maslach (1979), both *JPSP* | **Medium-high** | Both are partial/failed replications of key components. Verify which components |
| 7 | No clean autonomic signature maps onto each emotion category | Siegel et al. (2018), *Psychol Bull* 144:343–393, meta-analysis | **High** | **The load-bearing claim.** Strongest evidence in the film and the honest core of the thesis |
| 8 | The strong constructionist version — the body reacts and the brain invents a story after — is not established | Barrett (2017) vs. basic-emotion and affective-neuroscience counterviews | **Contested, stated as contested** | The film argues *against* the strong version. This is a safe direction to lean |
| 9 | Interpretation also draws on context, memory, learning and culture | Predictive-processing framing: Seth (2013), *TiCS*; Barrett & Simmons (2015), *Nat Rev Neurosci* 16:419–429 | **Medium** — live theory | Presented as the current framing, not as fact |

## Claims the film deliberately does **not** make

| Not said | Why |
|---|---|
| "Your emotions are constructed / invented" | Overclaims #8. The script says the opposite explicitly |
| "A racing heart *is* anxiety" | Reduces emotion to one signal. Contradicted by #7 |
| "Relabel anxiety as excitement and you'll perform better" | The reappraisal literature (Brooks 2014, *JEP:General* 143:1144–1158) is real but thin and single-lab. Named here so it is a *decision*, not an oversight. Using it as the payoff — right after a minute on replication failure — would be self-refuting |
| "The suspension bridge study proves misattribution" | Dutton & Aron (1974) is confounded and contested. **Cut from the film entirely** despite being the most film-friendly study available |
| Any figure for interoceptive accuracy | "Most people are bad at it" is defensible; a percentage is not, without checking the distribution |

## The honesty section, in full

Beat VII carries the load and must survive a hostile read. It concedes, in
order: the famous experiment did not replicate; the strong version of the idea
is wrong; the body is real but underdetermines the feeling. Only then does it
keep the weak version. That ordering is deliberate — the film earns the
conclusion by giving away everything it cannot defend first.
