# CAST — Selling Immortality

Counts are LAW and appear at point of use in every frame that stages the
character. NO character is a likeness of any real person (WORLD likeness
law); every named historical figure is staged as a period TYPE.

## Principals

**THE MAN** — the catalog's recurring protagonist, unchanged in every
century: a small slight man with a round face and a big confident nose, his
head bald and smooth except for a single lonely PAIR of hairs at the very
top of his crown — one thin hair kinking left, one leaning right — a flat
VERMILION RED coat with THREE round buttons, thin stick legs, ABSURDLY
oversized shoes. WANT: to not be done yet. He stands in EVERY era's queue
holding ONE blank ticket. THE WARM-MAN LAW: warm coat and face in any blue
evening.

**THE BARKER** — the booth's eternal salesman, same face every century in
different dress: a tall smiling man with a thin mustache, a dusty ORANGE
coat, ONE straw boater, one arm always PRESENTING (palm up toward the
product). Never sinister — warm, delighted, certain. He is the booth's
weather, not a villain.

**THE BULB-BOOTH** — the monument: ONE small wooden stall with ONE row of
warm round bulbs along its top edge, ONE counter shelf, re-dressed per era
(bunting 1889, clinic white 1925, pharmacy shelving 1930, glass boardroom
skin 2008, spa panels 2019). Always recognizably the same cart underneath.

**THE VIAL** — ONE small glass vial of softly glowing warm amber, the same
amber in every era; its descendants (bottle, bag, pill) keep the exact hue.

## Era company

**THE LECTURE HALL, 1889** — TWELVE bearded physicians in dark frock coats,
individuated faces and builds; ONE bearded lecturer TYPE at the lectern
holding ONE syringe (never a portrait of a real man); gaslight sconces.
**THE TICKET HOLDERS, 1925** — THREE rich old men in dark suits with canes,
each holding ONE blank ticket; ONE proud surgeon TYPE in a white coat; ONE
small monkey on a fence outside the window; ONE palm tree.
**THE PHARMACY, 1930** — ONE beaming pharmacist in a bow tie; a wall of
identical small bottles; ONE BLANK strongman poster; and for the straight
act: ONE believer at the counter (a composed period man, never comic).
**THE BOARDROOM, 2008** — SIX suited executives around ONE long table, ONE
blank contract with ONE ribbon; glass walls; the amber now inside ONE
molecule-shaped glass ornament on the table.
**THE LOUNGE, 2019** — FOUR recliners, FOUR IV stands, ONE bag of warm amber
liquid per stand, THREE relaxed clients, ONE white-coated attendant with ONE
blank tablet; floor-to-ceiling glass, ONE potted fig.
**THE PROTOCOL, today** — ONE trim man in plain grey (a TYPE, no likeness)
at ONE long table set with a wall of identical small bottles and ONE
pill-organizer grid drawn as blank compartments; morning light; clocks
showing (hands only, blank faces).
**THE REFEREE'S ROOM** — flat fluorescent light, steel racks, ONE white
mouse in ONE clear cage, ONE clipboard with BLANK ruled sheet; no bulbs, no
warmth — the only room the amber glow cannot enter.

## Environments (11 — booth/queue scenes capped at ≤30% combined)

1. the night street with the bulb-booth (the spine — every era's queue)
2. the 1889 lecture hall (gaslight)
3. the 1925 clinic (white tile, palm window)
4. the 1930 pharmacy (the straight act lives here)
5. the 2008 boardroom (PROSPECTUS accent)
6. the 2019 lounge (glass, dusk city)
7. the protocol kitchen/table, morning
8. the referee's mouse room (fluorescent — the anti-booth)
9. the apothecary product-wall (APOTHECARY accent frames)
10. the paper office (PROSPECTUS: the FDA page, the closed door)
11. void (flat cream field, connective tissue)

## Wardrobe / palette allocation

Vermilion is the Man's alone. Dusty orange = the Barker, every century. Ink
blue = evening fields + boardroom suits. Mustard = the booth light, the
bulbs, the amber's warm kin. Sage = clinic/lounge furnishings. Era-clock:
1889 frock coats and beards; 1925 white coats and canes; 1930 bow ties;
2008 grey suits; 2019 athleisure; today plain grey. The Man's red coat
never changes.

## Interaction quota

The queue is drawn with DIGNITY: individuated faces, the same small want,
no grotesques. The Barker and the Man share at least THREE frames across
eras (the transaction staged, both faces visible). The final act stages THE
TURN: the Man facing AWAY from the booth toward the queue, the queue drawn
as every era at once — coats and hats changing down the row, ONE ticket in
every hand. No frame mocks a buyer; the premise is staged (the glow, the
leaning), never the fool.
