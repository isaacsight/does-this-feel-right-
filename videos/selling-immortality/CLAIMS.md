# CLAIMS — Selling Immortality (episode 6, working title)

Topic chosen 2026-08-10, riding the David Sinclair JRE episode (#2537, aired
2026-08-07). The thesis: people have been selling the cure for aging for 140
years, and the pattern never changes — a charismatic scientist, a miraculous
substance, rich men first in line, and a quiet collapse nobody announces as
loudly as the launch. Verified rows only; every gag is a sourced fact.

## May NOT say

- Nothing that calls any living person a fraud. The pattern is the subject;
  the film shows rows 1-5 and lets the viewer notice the shape.
- No medical advice; no claim that any current intervention does or does not
  extend human life beyond what the cited record supports.
- "Rich men first in line" is the observed pattern in rows 1-5, not a moral
  verdict on any customer (the want — not dying — is universal and treated
  with respect; it is the same small want the catalog always honors).
- The Byers line is period-black-humor; his death itself is played straight.

## The spine (provisional, for the room)

The man's want, the largest yet somehow still small: he doesn't want to live
forever — he wants to not be done yet. And for 140 years, a booth has always
appeared next to that want: the elixir, the gland, the glowing water, the red
wine molecule, the young blood, the 111 pills before 11 AM. The pattern act
plays the five booths as one recurring stall with new paint. The straight act
is Byers. The mercy: the only interventions that ever survived the record are
boring, free, and — the joke history keeps making — mostly involve other
people (the grip film's receipt, the lonely chapter's Tuesday). The catalog
quietly closes its own loop: the fountain of youth was the lodge all along.

## Direction note (for the bake-off)

The recurring world holds a natural staging: THE BOOTH — one market stall
that re-dresses itself across five eras while the Man stands in the same
queue. Candidate directions: MIDWAY (carnival barker light), APOTHECARY
(shelf-and-bottle glow), PROSPECTUS (the pitch as paper — certificates,
prospectuses, all blank). Probe against: the 1889 injection announcement, the
monkey reserve, the Radithor shelf, the modern clinic.

## Direction (locked 2026-08-10): MIDWAY

Warmth as bait — the booth and the product glow warm mustard like a lit
window full of company while the world sits in cool ink blue; the crowd
leans toward the glow. THE BULB-BOOTH is this episode's monument object (the
catalog's third, after the slab and the phone): one bulb-lined stall,
re-dressed each era, recognizable every time. The amber vial is the SAME hue
in all five eras. Accents: APOTHECARY (order-as-authority: bottle walls,
frontal symmetry) for the product/authority frames; PROSPECTUS (paper as
promise AND as executioner — dusty auditor light) for the institutional act:
the $720M deal, the halted trial, the one-page FDA statement that ends
Ambrosia in hours.
