# PAGES — THE SKEPTIC · Act V only

## Angle

Write Act V as the strongest honest version of the argument that this whole
genre is a moral panic — not a straw version the film then flattens. The
prosecution the viewer has been assembling for three minutes gets cross-examined
by the film's own evidence first (Ward fails to replicate), then by the field's
biggest number (Orben), then by the history of the form itself (novels, radio,
television, games). The act ends on the one sentence that stops the steelman
becoming nihilism, and hands Act VI its job.

No self-flagellation, no confession register. Daniel is not sorry. He is
reading out the other side's case because the other side's case is good, and he
has already done the arithmetic on where it runs out.

Word count: **160** (act budget 141–160 at 150 wpm for 3:03–4:08).

## Pages

### ACT V — THE HONEST NUMBER · 3:03–4:08 · THE TURN

Here is where a film like this usually lies to you. We won't.

Start with our own evidence. That phone result failed a pre-registered repeat
in two thousand twenty-two. Twenty-two studies pooled together put the effect
at minus nought point one four. Real, and small.

Then the biggest study. Orben and Przybylski, three hundred and fifty-five
thousand adolescents, every defensible analysis run at once. Technology use
accounts for at most four tenths of one per cent of the variation in wellbeing.
Eating potatoes came in at nine tenths of that. Wearing glasses was one and a
half times worse.

And this film is the newest member of an old family. The novel was going to
ruin us. Then radio. Then television. Then video games.

That argument is good. It stops in one place. A small average is what a real
effect looks like when it is spread across people it never touches.

## Source notes

Line by line, in order of appearance.

1. **"That phone result failed a pre-registered repeat in two thousand
   twenty-two."**
   Ruiz Pardo, A. C., & Minda, J. P. (2022). *Reexamining the "brain drain"
   effect: A replication of Ward et al. (2017).* Acta Psychologica 230, 103717.
   Pre-registered direct replication of Ward Experiment 2; found no deleterious
   effect of phone presence on cognitive capacity. Already **VERIFIED** in the
   Director's source table, row 7 — I am reusing a checked citation, not
   introducing one.

2. **"Twenty-two studies pooled together put the effect at minus nought point
   one four."**
   Böttger, T., Poschik, M., & Zierer, K. (2023). *Does the Brain Drain Effect
   Really Exist? A Meta-Analysis.* Behavioral Sciences 13(9), 751. 22 studies,
   43 effects, pooled g = −0.14 (95% CI −0.24 to −0.03). **VERIFIED** in the
   Director's table, row 8. I have stated the pooled figure rather than
   characterising it, per the register.

3. **"Three hundred and fifty-five thousand adolescents… at most four tenths of
   one per cent of the variation in wellbeing… potatoes nine tenths… glasses
   one and a half times worse."**
   Orben, A., & Przybylski, A. K. (2019). *The association between adolescent
   well-being and digital technology use.* Nature Human Behaviour 3, 173–182.
   n = 355,358 across MTF, YRBS and MCS; specification curve; "at most 0.4% of
   the variation in well-being"; in-text: potatoes 0.9× (YRBS), glasses 1.5×
   (MCS). **VERIFIED** in the Director's table, row 9, with the manuscript read
   locally. Note I have rendered 0.9× as "nine tenths of that" rather than
   "almost as bad," because the ratio is the whole point and the paraphrase
   loses it.

4. **"The novel was going to ruin us. Then radio. Then television. Then video
   games."**
   Orben, A. (2020). *The Sisyphean Cycle of Technology Panics.* Perspectives on
   Psychological Science 15(5), 1143–1157. The paper's argument is that
   societal-level panics recur across new technologies — the novel, radio,
   television, video games — and that the research cycle restarts each time
   without accumulating. **NEW CITATION — DIRECTOR MUST VERIFY.** I have not
   opened the full text this session; I am supplying it from recall of the
   article and I would rather flag that than have it trusted one layer longer.
   If the volume or page range is wrong, the claim itself is still the
   sceptic's central historical move and can be re-sourced. If it cannot be
   verified in time, treat the four-sentence run as `[UNSOURCED]` and cut it —
   the act survives at 135 words.

   Deliberate note on what I did **not** write: I avoided the comics-code /
   Wertham beat and the eighteenth-century "reading rage" beat, both of which
   are more colourful and neither of which I can source to a paper I have read.

5. **"A small average is what a real effect looks like when it is spread across
   people it never touches."**
   Not a citation — a statistical statement about what a population mean is.
   It makes no empirical claim about the size of any subgroup effect, which is
   why it is phrased as a fact about averages rather than a claim about teenage
   girls or any other named group. If it is read as smuggling in an unsourced
   subgroup finding, cut the second half and land on "It stops in one place."

## Lines I would fight for

1. **"A small average is what a real effect looks like when it is spread across
   people it never touches."** This is the boundary the Director asked for. The
   steelman is unanswerable about the population and silent about the person,
   and that gap is exactly the door Act VI walks through. It also protects the
   film: the sceptic's case is granted in full, and the film still has
   somewhere to stand.

2. **"Real, and small."** Three words after two long sentences of machinery.
   It is the film's whole thesis compressed to the shortest line in the act,
   and it arrives before the viewer has been told how to feel about it.

3. **"Then the biggest study."** Four words, and it does the work that a
   paragraph of throat-clearing usually does. It also sets up the numbers to
   land unaccompanied, which is the only way the potato and the glasses are
   funny — the moment either is announced as absurd, it stops being absurd.

## Lines I am unsure of

- **"minus nought point one four."** Spoken aloud this is four beats of number
  with no unit the viewer can hold, and Hedges' g means nothing to a general
  audience. It obeys the chair's "say the number" rule and may still be the
  weakest three seconds in the act. Fallback that keeps the honesty and drops
  the opacity: *"Twenty-two studies pooled together found the effect real, and
  small."* I would take the fallback if Sound reports the number reads as
  mush.

- **"And this film is the newest member of an old family."** The self-reference
  is either the most disarming line in the act or the only place Daniel sounds
  like he has skin in the game — the register note forbids the latter. Safer
  variant that loses the disarmament: *"And this is an old family of
  argument."*

- **The four short historical sentences.** Read aloud they have a rhythm that
  edges toward list-comedy, which is a register the film does not have. Daniel
  will need to take them flat and even, with no lift on "video games." If that
  cannot be got in the booth, cut to two: *"The novel was going to ruin us.
  Then television."*

## Structural notes

Written as notes, not enacted. The act order in TREATMENT.md is unchanged in my
pages.

1. **The order inside Act V is doing load-bearing work and should not be
   resequenced.** Own evidence first, field evidence second, history third,
   boundary last. If history moves before the numbers, the act reads as an
   opinion looking for support. Where it sits now, the viewer has already
   conceded the numbers and the history lands as explanation rather than
   argument.

2. **Act VI is now carrying more weight than 58 words may bear.** My version of
   Act V grants the sceptic more than the Director's draft does, which means
   Festinger has to be the thing that survives the granting. As written, Act VI
   asserts that comparison "is not small" without a magnitude — the one act in
   the film that names an effect and does not size it, immediately after an act
   whose entire subject is sizing effects. A hostile viewer will notice. Two
   honest options: give Act VI a number, or change "not small" to a claim about
   the *environment* having changed, which is observational and needs no
   effect size. The second is cheaper and the Director's own Dunbar cut already
   points that way.

3. **The turn arrives on line two of my draft, not line one.** "We won't" is
   the promise; "That phone result failed" is the turn. If Sound wants the
   3:03 marker to sit on the turn itself rather than the promise, the act break
   may need to move about four seconds earlier.
