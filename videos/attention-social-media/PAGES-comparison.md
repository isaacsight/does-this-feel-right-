# PAGES — COMPARISON, WITHOUT THE NUMBER

**Writer's angle · filed for the Director's selection pass**
Act VI, card C5, card C6. Nothing else touched.

---

## Angle

Act VI has to be the one claim the film does not walk back, and it has to make
that claim with no statistic underneath it. Dunbar is retired, so the act
cannot borrow authority from a number. It has to borrow it from recognition
instead: the viewer should feel the comparison running in their own chest
before the narrator finishes describing it.

The structural burden is trust. The film has just spent sixty-five seconds
taking its own evidence apart. Act VI cannot arrive louder than Act V or the
whole honesty move reads as a setup. So: Festinger stated plainly, once, with
no adjective on it — then a single observational turn about the environment,
which requires no citation because it is not a contested finding. The act
gets its weight from the drop in scale, not from a figure.

Register held: warm, a little sad, not accusing. The last two lines are about
the viewer's situation, never the viewer's fault.

## Pages

### ACT VI — THE TRIBE AT SCALE · 4:08–4:35 · 69 words

> The part that is not small is comparison. Leon Festinger, nineteen
> fifty-four. People work out how they are doing by looking at other people.
> Where there is no objective yardstick, they use whoever is available.
>
> For most of your life, that meant the people in the room. The room is now
> everyone, ranked, at their best, and never closed. Nobody asked you to enter
> it. You are in it.

**Word count:** 69. Budget was ~68. Two paragraphs, one breath each in the
first, four sentences in the second running long-long-short-short so the act
lands on the four-word line.

**A note on "ranked."** First draft had "sorted." Cut it — for a British
narrator "sorted" carries a colloquial second meaning (*fine, dealt with*) and
the ear may take the wrong one at speed. "Ranked" is unambiguous and it is the
literal mechanism.

---

### CARD C5 — after Act V

> Real. Documented. Smaller than we said.

Six words. The original card said *smaller than you were told*, which puts the
error outside the film. Act V no longer permits that: the film retracts its own
Act IV before it touches anybody else's work, so the card has to include the
film in the correction. Changing one pronoun does all of it — **we**, not
**you**. It keeps the three-beat rhythm the Editor already has type set for.

**Alternates, in order of preference:**

1. `Real. Documented. Smaller than we said.` *(6 — primary)*
2. `Ours was wrong too. Still real.` *(6)*
3. `Corrected twice. Ours first.` *(4 — shortest; loses "real")*

---

### CARD C6 — after Act VI

> No measure but each other.

Five words. This is Festinger stated as a condition rather than a finding —
Hypothesis II with the citation apparatus taken off. It closes the act without
a number, without a ceiling, and without a villain. Read cold it is a small sad
sentence about people, which is the correct exit temperature for this act.

**Alternates, in order of preference:**

1. `No measure but each other.` *(5 — primary)*
2. `We measure by each other.` *(5)*
3. `The only yardstick is other people.` *(6)*

## Lines I would fight for

1. **"The room is now everyone, ranked, at their best, and never closed."**
   This is the whole act. The pivot lives in the repeated word *room* — the
   viewer is given something small and warm, and then the same word is handed
   back at a size the body cannot process. That is the vertigo the intent note
   asks for, and it costs no statistic.
2. **"You are in it."** Four words, and the shortest sentence in the film after
   "We won't." It is the landing the act has been descending toward. If the
   Director needs to cut for time, cut the sentence before it, not this one.
3. **Card C6, "No measure but each other."** It closes Act VI on exactly the
   ground the film can defend, and it is the only card in the picture that is
   sad rather than dry.

---

## Lines I am unsure of

- **"Nobody asked you to enter it."** This is the one line in my pages that
  could be heard as accusation-adjacent — it sits half a step from *they did
  this to you*, which is the register the film has spent five minutes refusing.
  I think it stays on the right side because the grammar makes nobody the
  agent. But it is the line I would test first in Daniel's read, and if it
  performs at all, cut it and let "You are in it" follow "never closed"
  directly. That version runs 63 words and still works.
- **"yardstick."** Concrete and good for the ear, but faintly folksy against
  the film's vocabulary. "Objective measure" is flatter and safer; I chose the
  object over the abstraction per house discipline. Director's call.
- **"The part that is not small is comparison."** I kept the Director's
  existing opener verbatim because it is load-bearing as a hinge from Act V.
  It is also the least interesting sentence in my pages. I could not improve it
  without breaking the join, and the join matters more.

## Structural notes

*Written as notes. Nothing below is enacted in the pages.*

**1. The act table and the brief disagree on length.** The table gives Act VI
58 words; the assignment gave me 68. I wrote to 69. If 58 is the real number,
the cut is the "Nobody asked you to enter it" sentence plus "and never closed,"
which lands at 58 exactly and keeps every load-bearing beat. Flagging rather
than guessing.

**2. Act VI's first line is a dependency, not a sentence.** "The part that is
not small" only parses because Act V ended on smallness. If Act V's closing
paragraph gets trimmed in the merge — particularly "the population-level effect
is small and badly measured" — this opener loses its antecedent and will need
rewriting. Worth checking after Act V is locked, not before.

**3. Shot 24 — intent only, the Art Director composes it.** The tiling grid was
built to show a ceiling being exceeded, and there is no longer a ceiling. What
the shot now has to do is the opposite of counting. The feeling wanted is the
intimate thing from shot 23 losing its edge — not multiplied into a countable
array, but continuing past any point where counting would begin, so the viewer
gives up on the arithmetic rather than completing it. A repeating grid is the
wrong instinct now, because a grid of discrete units is a number waiting to be
estimated, and the film has just finished saying it does not have that number.
Warmth should survive the scale change; this is loss of edge, not menace. Held
just long enough that the eye searches for a boundary and does not find one,
and then out — the intent note says the act must not linger past its welcome.

**4. Cards C5 and C6 now rhyme, and I did that deliberately.** C5 admits the
film's own error; C6 admits a condition nobody is exempt from. Both put the
narrator inside the frame rather than above it. If the Editor sets them in the
same weight, the pair reads as one gesture across the turn. If the Director
would rather they not rhyme, take alternate 2 for C5.
