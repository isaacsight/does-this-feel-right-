# STYLE STUDY — Zenn (@Zenn0009) and the crude-explainer genre

> Researched 2026-07-23 by direct study of Zenn's videos (The Marshmallow
> Test, The Dunning-Kruger Effect) plus genre survey. This supersedes the
> "pure whiteboard, black-on-white" assumption. Read before building the
> character-driven rebuild.

## What Zenn actually is (from the frames, not from memory)

The earlier read — "black line on pure white, one red accent" — was only half
right. Studying two videos directly shows a richer, warmer style:

1. **Characters are thin STICK FIGURES with round, expressive heads.** Single-
   line arms and legs; a plain circle head with dot eyes and a simple mouth
   that carries real emotion (skeptical, mesmerized, content). NOT a pear-
   bodied cartoon person. The Dunning-Kruger lead is a classic stick figure
   carrying a money bag.

2. **Muted flat COLOR, not black-on-white.** Backgrounds are filled with a
   limited, warm, desaturated palette — tans, muted yellows, soft greys, a
   gold — with confident black outlines over the top. The Marshmallow frame
   read mostly white because that scene was simple (one plate, one
   marshmallow); the Dunning-Kruger bank interior is a full muted-color set.
   The look is flat-fill storybook/comic, not marker-on-whiteboard.

3. **Full environments the character acts in.** A bank with a door, a window,
   a car outside, a counter, columns. Legible, literal SETS — not floating
   icons on a void. This is what kills ambiguity: you always know where you
   are and what is happening.

4. **Real, limited character animation.** The figure walks, gestures, reacts —
   full-body 2D motion, not a camera drift over a still. The "fun to watch"
   comes from a character DOING things, plainly and a little comically.

5. **Hand-lettered emphasis as art.** A big marker word with an arrow ("NOW")
   drawn into the scene on a key beat — not typeset cards. Sparing.

6. **One idea per shot, always legible.** Every frame is a single clear
   readable action. Nothing abstract, nothing to decode.

## The voice (narration)

- Plain, present- or past-tense, second person. Talks TO you.
- Narrator is a person with a point of view — allowed to say "I."
- Hook-forward: the Dunning-Kruger description is *"There is one question
  about yourself that you are built to get wrong, and asking it harder does
  not help."* Intriguing, plain, slightly ominous, zero throat-clearing.
- Dry, wry, never lofty or sloganeering. Marshmallow: *"You can eat this one
  now, or you can wait."*
- ~8-minute videos; #psychology #documentary #humanbehavior. Our subject
  (attention, behaviour science) is dead-center Zenn's lane — the channel has
  a Marshmallow Test and a Dunning-Kruger already.

## The genre it belongs to

Crude-but-charming educational storytelling with simple 2D animation and a
strong narrative voice. Reference siblings and what each contributes:

- **Sam O'Nella Academy** — deliberately crude MS-Paint-tier art, fast dry wit,
  dense facts. The proof that rough art + great timing beats polish.
- **Oversimplified** — simple flat-color cartoon figures, full scenes, running
  visual gags, history as story. Closest to Zenn's flat-color-scene look.
- **CGP Grey** — effortless stick figures, tight scripts, one clear idea at a
  time, relentless clarity.
- **TheOdd1sOut / Jaiden** — expressive simple characters, personal wry voice,
  relatability over render quality.
- **After Skool** — the elegant continuous-drawing end of the same family
  (black marker on white, symbolic, contemplative). Our earlier reference; it
  is the *serious* cousin of Zenn's *fun* register.

Common denominator across all of them, and the lesson for us: **the audience
watches for the character, the voice, and the pace — never the render.** Rough
and legible and funny beats beautiful and ambiguous and calm, every time.

## COMEDY MANDATE — added 2026-07-24, overrides everything below

Isaac's note on the first stills cut: **"needs more fun and comedy, and some
images don't make sense."** Root cause found: the old style block literally
prompted *"calm, comforting, quiet."* We were commanding restraint on every
frame and then wondering why nothing was funny. The model obeyed us.

**The style block is replaced. Use this verbatim on every frame:**

> Crude, funny hand-drawn cartoon in the style of a comedic explainer channel.
> Thin stick-figure character with a round head and a BIG exaggerated
> expression — wide bulging eyes, dropped jaw, raised brows, or a flat deadpan
> stare, whichever the joke needs. Cartoon reaction conventions welcome: sweat
> drops, motion lines, squiggles, wobble lines, exaggerated posture. Muted flat
> colour set with confident black outlines — warm tans, muted yellows, soft
> greys. Exactly ONE flat tomato-red element as the accent. Comic, energetic,
> absurd situation played completely straight. Legible at a glance.
> 16:9. No lettering, no words, no numerals.

**Four comedy rules for scene writing:**

1. **Situations, not symbols.** A funny thing HAPPENING beats a metaphor. The
   two frames that already worked are the potato-and-spectacles deadpan and
   the thumb-back-at-the-clock aside — both concrete, both absurd, both played
   straight. Every abstract scene gets rewritten into an event.
2. **The hero must REACT.** He is the comedy. Give him a face and a posture in
   every frame: smug, wrecked, hopeful, humiliated, dead-eyed, delighted.
   Never neutral. Neutral is what made the first pass dull.
3. **Absurd played straight is the house joke.** Nobody in frame acknowledges
   the ridiculousness. The pigeon is treated as a serious colleague. The
   potato is presented as evidence.
4. **Escalate.** If a beat recurs (the phone, the badge, the scroll), make it
   bigger and dumber each time it comes back.

**Never again in a prompt:** calm, comforting, quiet, serene, contemplative,
gentle, restrained, elegant, tasteful. Those words produced three rejected
versions of this film.

## Best practices distilled (what we adopt)

1. **Follow one character.** A single stick-figure everyperson through the
   whole film gives the connective spine we structurally lacked.
2. **Muted flat-color sets, black outline.** Warm limited palette; keep our
   tomato-red as the reward/attention accent within it.
3. **Character DOES the beat.** Every science point is acted out literally by
   the figure in a set — never illustrated as an abstract symbol.
4. **Hook in the first line.** Open on intrigue aimed at the viewer, no setup.
5. **Plain wry voice, narrator as a person.** The reworked script (TREATMENT
   revision five) already does this.
6. **Pace and legibility over polish.** One clear idea per shot; cut on the
   beat; let rough linework be charming, not a defect to sand away.
7. **Sparing hand-lettered emphasis** on the few biggest beats only.
