# PAGES — ZENN VOICE, CHARACTER-ACTION FRAMING (all seven acts)

**Angle brief:** Same rework, but every line must sit over ONE everyperson
figure doing a literal, legible thing in crude line animation. The narration
explains while the character does. Write the VO so the verb the viewer hears is
close to the action the viewer sees — pick up, check, scroll, reach, compare,
put down. Keep every verified claim. I'll note the on-screen action beside each
beat.

## Lines I am unsure of
- I lean harder on the character than the plain writer does ("watch what your
  thumb does"). It risks getting cute. Kept the cuteness only where it earns a
  gesture.
- Act V is the hard one for a character film — the retraction is abstract. My
  solution: the character physically sets the phone down and we pull back to
  find them as one figure in a huge crowd. The line "people it never touched"
  becomes literal — most of the crowd never picked up anything.
- I kept "you're in it" as the Act VI landing because it pays off a wide shot.

## Pages (line → action)

**I.**
- "Watch your hand for a second. You wake up, and before your eyes are open,
  it already knows where the phone is." → figure asleep, arm reaches off-frame,
  the one red glow of a phone.
- "An economist named Herbert Simon called this in 1971. Looking at information
  costs you attention. More information, less attention to go around." → figure
  sitting in a vast empty room, papers piling up, getting smaller.
- "Which makes your attention scarce. And scarce things have a price." → the
  red phone glow sits alone in the empty room.
- "Fifty-five years later, somebody's buying yours. The sale closes in
  milliseconds." → a hand offscreen closes over the glow.

**II.**
- "You've heard the phone gives you dopamine. Close. Also wrong where it
  counts." → figure taps the phone, waits.
- "In 1997, Schultz, Dayan and Montague found what dopamine actually tracks.
  Not pleasure. Surprise. The gap between what you expected and what you got." →
  figure taps again; nothing; taps again; a red burst.
- "Get what you predicted, nothing much. Get what you didn't, and it spikes." →
  flat, flat, flat, then the figure jolts at the red burst.
- "So it isn't built to make you happy. It's built to keep you guessing." →
  figure keeps tapping, leaning in.

**III.**
- "That guessing has a name. In 1957, Ferster and Skinner mapped what happens
  when the reward comes — but not every time, and never on a pattern you can
  crack." → figure at a little lever, pulling.
- "Of everything they tested, hardest habit to break. A pigeon fed at random
  keeps pecking long after a pigeon fed on schedule wanders off." → the figure
  pulls the lever like a bird pecking.
- "Now look at your own phone. Pull to refresh — slot machine lever. The red
  badge, which tells you nothing except that something happened. And the feed
  with no bottom, which took the one thing every page used to have. An end." →
  figure pulls-to-refresh, a badge pops, then scrolls down and down, the feed
  never ends, the figure shrinks.
- "It was on purpose. In 2017, one of Facebook's founders said it out loud:
  they built a social-approval loop that exploits a weakness in people. And, his
  words, they did it anyway." → figure still scrolling, small, in the dark.

**IV.**
- "So what's it cost you? Not in outrage. In numbers." → figure at a desk,
  trying to work, phone face-down beside them.
- "Sophie Leroy, 2009: get pulled off a task before you finish, and part of
  your head stays behind. Attention residue." → figure tries to read; a faint
  red thread ties their head back to the phone.
- "Adrian Ward's team, 2017: memory tests with the phone on the desk, in a
  pocket, in another room. Some switched it fully off." → figure glances at the
  face-down phone, glances again.
- "Switching it off did nothing. Distance did. Further away, the more they
  could hold." → figure reaches for the phone and stops, hand hovering.
- "It doesn't have to buzz. It just has to be close enough to grab." → hand
  hovers over the still, dark phone.

**V.**
- "Okay. That last study is the weakest thing I've shown you." → figure sets
  the phone down and looks up.
- "In 2022 someone ran it again — analysis locked in before they saw the data.
  No effect." → we start to pull back from the figure.
- "I just spent thirty-five seconds on that." → dry; the figure is now smaller
  in frame.
- "A year later, a review pooled twenty-two studies. Real effect — about a
  seventh of a standard deviation. Small." → pull-back continues; more figures
  appear around ours.
- "The biggest look we have: Orben and Przybylski, 2019. Every reasonable
  analysis at once, across three hundred and fifty-five thousand teenagers. The
  link is real, negative, and at most four tenths of one percent of the story.
  In the same data, eating potatoes looked almost as bad. Glasses, one and a
  half times worse. And in one dataset alone, six hundred million defensible ways
  to run it." → the crowd fills the frame; our figure is one dot.
- "To be clear, this isn't me letting them off. The tricks are real. The harm,
  across everyone, is small and hard to measure. Both true." → the whole crowd
  visible.
- "A tiny average is what a real effect looks like when you spread it across
  millions of people it never touched." → most of the crowd never held a phone
  at all; only a few glow red.

**VI.**
- "Leon Festinger, 1954: you figure out how you're doing by looking at other
  people. No objective yardstick, you use whoever's around." → our figure back
  in a small circle, faces turned toward each other.
- "For most of history, that meant the people in the room." → the small warm
  circle.
- "Now the room is everyone. Ranked. On their best day. Never closed." → the
  circle explodes outward into an endless wall of glowing windows.
- "And you're in it." → our figure alone against the wall, looking up.

**VII.**
- "None of this is a conspiracy. It's a business model, right there in the
  filings. They sell your attention, over and over, and what keeps it worth
  selling is you never quite know what's next." → figure holds the phone, the
  red glow.
- "You won't out-willpower it. Nobody does. So change the machine, not
  yourself. Turn off the badges. Make the rewards predictable. Boring." → figure
  taps a setting; the red badge goes grey.
- "And then it's easy to put down. Because there's nothing left to wait for." →
  figure sets the phone face-down, stands, the room warms, evening.
