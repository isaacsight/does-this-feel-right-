### Style block — replaced, prefixed verbatim to every scene prompt

> Crude, funny hand-drawn cartoon in the style of a comedic explainer channel.
> Thin stick-figure character with a round head and a BIG exaggerated expression
> — wide bulging eyes, dropped jaw, raised brows, or a flat deadpan stare,
> whichever the joke needs. Cartoon reaction conventions welcome: sweat drops,
> motion lines, squiggles, wobble lines, exaggerated posture. Muted flat colour
> set with confident black outlines — warm tans, muted yellows, soft greys.
> Exactly ONE flat tomato-red element as the accent. Comic, energetic, absurd
> situation played completely straight. Legible at a glance. 16:9. No lettering,
> no words, no numerals. Keep the same protagonist as the reference image.

## World

**Warm e-ink daylight — plain objects, held still, one red thing in every
frame.**

A stranger should be able to execute from that sentence. The surface is a
warm off-white e-ink page: matte, non-glossy, low contrast, very fine paper
grain and no print artifacts. The objects are the ones that were true in 1900
and will be true in 2100 — hands, paper, water, thread, seed, stone, stairs,
cloth, cord, glass, a book, weather, evening light. Light is soft and ambient
with no harsh shadow. Nothing in frame carries a decade.

**The one-accent rule is the film's focal discipline.** Two red objects in a
frame is a defect and gets retaken. Where a motif recurs — the thread, the
ember, the ribbon, the dot — the red is the *same* red at the *same* weight,
because continuity of the accent across five minutes is the thing a viewer
registers without being able to name it.

faded tomato, part-way to grey. That is the shot's entire subject and it is

## Shot 24 — re-conceived, not re-propped

The Director endorsed the comparison writer's note and folded it into intent:
**the shot's job is now loss of edge, not multiplication.** The Dunbar 150 is
cut from the film, so there is no ceiling left to exceed, and the treatment is
explicit that "an image that invites the eye to estimate a quantity here would
hand back the argument."

The old concept — the campfire circle tiled into a grid receding to the
horizon — is dead, and it is dead for the argument's reason rather than a
stylistic one. **A grid of discrete units is a number waiting to be
estimated,** and the film has just spent seventy seconds saying it does not
have that number. A viewer counting circles in Act VI is a viewer the film
handed a magnitude to after refusing to name one.

The replacement keeps the shot-23 motif and takes its boundary away. The
intimate ring stays sharp at the near centre; its far side is simply gone, and
the seated forms continue outward in every direction, losing separability as
they go until they are a warm continuous field of shoulders. There is no
horizon, no repeat, no unit and no edge. The eye goes looking for where the
crowd stops and does not find it, then gives up on the arithmetic instead of
completing it — which is precisely the effect the note asked for.

Three constraints held:

- **Warmth survives the scale change.** The field is warm, not menacing. Same
  ember, same light, same paper.
- **Nothing is countable.** No repeated element, no lattice, no receding row.
- **The motif carries.** Shot 23's ember is shot 24's ember and shot 25's
  reflected ember. Object continuity across the act is checked at the review
  gate.
