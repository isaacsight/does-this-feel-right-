# PAGES — THE RETRACTION (Act V)

**Writer's angle brief:** Act V only, 3:03–4:08, ~160 words. Make the film's
self-retraction land as an event rather than an erratum. Demonstrate the
honesty instead of announcing it; let the withdrawal cost something visible;
land the four load-bearing numbers without listing them.

---

## Angle, in my own words

Act IV asks the viewer to believe something. Act V takes it away. The current
draft opens by promising not to lie, which is a claim about the film's
character — and a film that has to say it is honest has already spent the
credit it was trying to earn. My version opens by simply doing the thing: the
first sentence downgrades the film's own best evidence, before the viewer has
been told anything about integrity.

The emotional shape I am after is not drama. It is the small internal
adjustment of putting a belief down — you were persuaded thirty seconds ago,
the persuasion has been withdrawn, and nobody raised their voice about it. So
the act names the cost out loud once ("the film just spent forty seconds on
that") and then keeps moving, calmly, to the bigger number.

Numbers are placed so each one arrives alone and unqualified. No adjective
does work a figure can do.

## Pages

### ACT V — THE HONEST NUMBER · 3:03–4:08 · THE TURN

> That last result is the weakest thing in this film.
>
> In two thousand twenty-two, a pre-registered replication ran the experiment
> again, with the analysis declared before the data came in. Phones on the
> desk. Phones in another room. No difference.
>
> The film just spent forty seconds on that.
>
> A meta-analysis the year after pooled twenty-two studies. The effect
> survives, at about a seventh of a standard deviation. Too small to feel.
>
> Amy Orben and Andrew Przybylski, two thousand nineteen. Every defensible
> analysis at once, across three hundred and fifty-five thousand adolescents.
> The association is negative, real, and at most four tenths of one per cent
> of the variation in wellbeing. In the same data, eating potatoes was almost
> as bad. Wearing glasses was worse.
>
> That is not a defence of the industry. The mechanisms are real. The harm,
> across a population, is small and badly measured. Both are true. Only one of
> them sells a documentary.

**Word count: 156**, against a 160 budget. At 150 wpm that is 62 seconds
spoken, leaving about three seconds of the 65-second slot for the paragraph
breaks, which are real breaths in this act and should not be compressed.

## Source notes

Per line, in order. All rows correspond to entries the Director has already
verified in the `TREATMENT.md` source table; I have introduced no new claim
that is not already in that table, except where marked.

1. **"That last result is the weakest thing in this film."**
   Editorial judgement, not a factual claim. It characterises the film's own
   evidence, and the characterisation is supported by rows 7 and 8 below.

2. **"a pre-registered replication ran the experiment again… No difference."**
   Ruiz Pardo, A. C., & Minda, J. P. (2022). *Reexamining the "brain drain"
   effect: A replication of Ward et al. (2017).* Acta Psychologica 230,
   103717. Pre-registered direct replication of Ward Experiment 2; found no
   deleterious effect of phone presence. TREATMENT source table row 7,
   VERIFIED.
   *Writer's note:* "Phones on the desk. Phones in another room." restates the
   Ward Experiment 2 location manipulation the film established in Act IV, so
   the replication is heard as a repeat of the same test, not a new one. No
   power/off condition is mentioned here, correctly — that belongs to Act IV.

3. **"The film just spent forty seconds on that."**
   Internal, self-referential. Act IV is 2:24–3:03 in the treatment's act
   table = 39 seconds. If Sound's measured read moves Act IV outside the
   35–45s band, this line needs its number changed or the line cut. Flagging
   it as a dependency rather than a source.

4. **"A meta-analysis the year after pooled twenty-two studies. The effect
   survives, at about a seventh of a standard deviation."**
   Böttger, T., Poschik, M., & Zierer, K. (2023). *Does the Brain Drain Effect
   Really Exist? A Meta-Analysis.* Behavioral Sciences 13(9), 751. 22 studies,
   43 effects, pooled g = −0.14 (95% CI −0.24 to −0.03). TREATMENT row 8,
   VERIFIED. "About a seventh of a standard deviation" is a plain-language
   restatement of g = −0.14 — Hedges' g is a standardised mean difference, and
   0.14 is one seventh to two decimal places. "The year after" is 2022 → 2023,
   correct.
   *Writer's note:* "Too small to feel" is my gloss, not the paper's. It is
   defensible for an effect of that size in a between-subjects cognitive task,
   but it is an inference and the Director should treat it as one. If it will
   not survive verification, "Real, and very small." is the safe swap and
   costs two words.

5. **"Amy Orben and Andrew Przybylski, two thousand nineteen… four tenths of
   one per cent… potatoes… glasses."**
   Orben, A., & Przybylski, A. K. (2019). *The association between adolescent
   well-being and digital technology use.* Nature Human Behaviour 3, 173–182.
   Specification curve across three datasets, total n = 355,358; technology
   use accounts for at most 0.4% of the variation in well-being; potatoes
   0.9× the association, glasses 1.5×, both in the manuscript text rather than
   press coverage. TREATMENT row 9 plus the potato note, VERIFIED, full text
   read by the Director.
   *Writer's note:* I dropped the draft's framing phrase "ran the
   specification curve" and kept only what it means — "every defensible
   analysis at once." One fewer piece of jargon for the ear to carry, same
   claim.

6. **"That is not a defence of the industry… Only one of them sells a
   documentary."**
   Editorial. The two component claims — mechanisms documented, population
   effect small — are carried by rows 1–5 and 7–9 of the treatment table
   respectively.

## Lines I would fight for

1. **"That last result is the weakest thing in this film."**
   This is the whole assignment in one sentence. It performs the retraction
   instead of promising one, it puts the film's own work in the dock before
   anyone else's, and it gives Daniel something he can say completely flat.
   It also means the act opens with a downgrade rather than a boast, which is
   the only version of this beat a sceptical viewer will believe.

2. **"The film just spent forty seconds on that."**
   The emotional beat lives here. It is the film acknowledging that the
   withdrawal has a price and that it is the one paying it. Without a line
   like this the retraction is a fact; with it, it is a decision. It is also
   the shortest sentence in the act, which is where the air comes from.

3. **"In the same data, eating potatoes was almost as bad. Wearing glasses was
   worse."**
   Not mine — it is the Director's, from the potato note — but it is the line
   the act is built to arrive at and I would fight anyone who trimmed it to
   one clause. Potatoes alone can be read as cherry-picking. Glasses closes
   that door and does it in five words.

---

## Lines I am unsure of

- **"Too small to feel."** My gloss on g = −0.14, discussed above. It is the
  most quotable phrase in my draft, which by my own rule makes it the most
  likely to have been reached for rather than earned. Cut it if verification
  is at all uncomfortable.
- **"about a seventh of a standard deviation"** is arithmetically clean but
  may be a beat of maths too far for the ear in an act that already carries
  three other figures. The alternative is to say "0.14" and let the smallness
  do its own work, but a bare decimal is worse out loud. I have kept the
  fraction; I would not argue hard for it.
- **"Every defensible analysis at once"** assumes the viewer can infer what a
  specification curve is from the phrase. I think it lands. I am not certain
  it lands on one hearing.

## Structural notes

Written as notes. I have not enacted any of them.

1. **My draft removes "Here is where a film like this usually lies to you. We
   won't."** The Register section names "We won't" as the two most important
   words in the film and gives Sound a specific direction for them. That
   direction now has nothing to attach to. My argument for the removal is in
   the angle above — it is a virtue claim, and Act V is strong enough to make
   the same point by acting instead of asserting. But this is a direct
   collision with a locked note, and the Director should resolve it
   deliberately rather than by picking the prettier paragraph. If "We won't"
   stays, my opening line should go; the two cannot both be first.

2. **Order within the act.** I have kept the treatment's order — own evidence
   first, field second — and I think it is right. Retracting Ward before
   reaching for Orben means the film has already paid before it asks the
   viewer to accept a larger correction, and the Orben numbers land on a
   viewer who has just watched the film cost itself something.

3. **Card C5.** The treatment flags that C5 now sits under a doubled
   correction. If the Editor wants copy from this angle, the honest version is
   two beats rather than one clever one: the film's own finding did not hold,
   and the field's finding is small. I have not written card copy here because
   my brief was Act V VO only, but I will file it if the Director wants it.

4. **A dependency, not a complaint.** Line 3 carries a hard number about the
   film's own runtime. It is the only line in my pages that can be falsified
   by the edit rather than by a paper. Sound records first, so it will be
   known early; it just has to be checked at that moment and not later.
