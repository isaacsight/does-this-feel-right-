## The two hard structural rules

**1. Comedy stops at 4:38 and does not come back until 5:52.**
Act VII is plain rooms, plain shots, generous holds, no archive. The previous
film earned its honesty section by shutting up first, and that is the single
most transferable thing it did. Act IX is allowed one gag because it is a
callback, not a joke.

**2. The peak is staged under a conditional.**
Act VI is the biggest, silliest sequence in the film, and every frame of it
sits under *"if it's right."* That word is what licenses the metaphor and what
lets Act VII concede the mechanism without the film collapsing. **If an edit
drops the conditional, Act VI becomes an assertion of Tier 4 and the claims
table is broken.** Check this at Gate 5.

## Recurring-gag plan

**One metaphor. The archive is the only mechanism in the film** — nothing else
gets personified, and no second workplace appears.

| Stage | Beat | Gag | What it clarifies |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup | III | An archivist fetches a folder; the "reel" inside is a set-building kit | Storage is notes, not recordings |
| Escalation A | IV | A gap is filled from a box of generic parts | Reconstruction fills gaps plausibly |
| Escalation B | IV | An archivist references a photograph from the wrong decade | Later information enters |
| Escalation C | IV | The neighbour's dog is placed into a childhood birthday. Nobody objects | Plausible ≠ true |
| Escalation D | VI | Two versions of one event argue over which is original | No internal marker distinguishes them |
| **Peak** | VI | The protagonist asks for the original. Every archivist finds something else to look at | There is no original to produce |
| Reset | VII | Workshop dark, empty, one lamp | — |
| Callback | IX | ORIGINAL → CURRENT VERSION; the objection is filed under ALSO A MEMORY | The objection is itself reconstructed |

**The gate:** every gag must clarify the mechanism, expose the objection, or set
up a callback. A frame that does none is cut. Additionally — and this is the
rule specific to this episode — **any gag that only works if reconsolidation is
true gets cut**, because the archive is licensed by Tier 1, not Tier 4.

Two gags from the brief are held in reserve rather than dropped: the box marked
PROBABLY and the folder marked THINGS MUM SAID HAPPENED. Both are strong, both
are pure text, and text is composited after generation — so they cost
compositing time, not generation risk. Use whichever the cut has room for.

## Silliness calibration

The previous film shipped at 64% literal · 14% object · 10% physics · 10%
anchor · 2% flourish. Target here, for the briefed ~25% increase:

> **50% literal · 12% object · 16% physics · 16% archive · 6% flourish**

Literal stays the majority and the gate is unchanged: *can the viewer be wrong
about what they're seeing?* If yes, it must be literal. The added silliness is
spent almost entirely inside the archive — one location the viewer has been
taught to read — which is why it reads as more absurd rather than less clear.

---

## Visual anchor

**The kitchen.** Returns in I, IV, VI and IX. Same room, same camera position,
same red mug beside the sink — the curtains are the variable. Blue in the
remembered version, absent in the true version, and mid-repaint whenever the
archive is working on it.

This is the direct descendant of the polyvagal chair: one frame a lost viewer
can always re-enter on. The mug carries over from that film as a deliberate
continuity object between episodes.

## Red discipline

Red is **evidence, correction and contamination** — never decoration. The mug,
the correction strip, the wrong-decade photograph's marker, the CURRENT VERSION
label. If it is red, the archive did it. Gate 5 verification checks this
explicitly.
