# YOUR MEMORY IS NOT THE ORIGINAL — v2

**~5:00.** Shorter than v1's 6:42 — v1 was slack and the archive did craft work
instead of jokes. Same research, same claims table, same honesty section.

## What changed and why

v1 failed on two counts and both were mine.

**It wasn't funny because nothing impossible happened.** The polyvagal film ran
on THE FORMULA — *a situation an ordinary person has lived through, resolved by
an impossible cartoon consequence.* I dropped it. v1's archivists quietly do
joinery. That is production design, not comedy.

**It didn't make sense because the camera was too far away.** 22 of 62 frames
were the same wide workshop with small figures doing small actions. You cannot
read a joke at that scale, and there is no human situation to recognise.

**The fix in one line:** the film is no longer *about an archive*. It is about
**a person losing an argument they are certain they are winning** — and the
archive is why they keep losing.

## The new engine: the argument

Every act, the protagonist states something with total confidence and is
immediately, physically contradicted. That is a shape everyone has lived. The
archive is the *reason*, revealed underneath, not the subject.

**Relatability anchors — one per act, all domestic:**
arguing about a holiday · the family story told at every gathering · swearing
you turned the oven off · remembering your own childhood kitchen wrong ·
insisting to a sibling that it happened *your* way.

## Comedy rules for the frames

1. **THE FORMULA, restored verbatim.** Recognisable setup, impossible payoff.
   Bodies deflate, jaws detach, someone leaves a person-shaped hole. Consequences
   enormous, instant, wildly out of proportion.
2. **Get close.** Mid-shots and reaction shots of the protagonist. The archive
   is background. Wide establishing shots: **three in the whole film**, no more.
3. **The archivists are bad colleagues, not craftsmen.** They eat lunch over the
   evidence, take a call mid-repair, file things while looking away, and have
   clearly stopped caring. That is funnier and truer than diligent restorers.
4. **The protagonist is contradicted in the same frame, never a cut later.**
   Their face changes while they are still mid-sentence.
5. Target mix: **20% cartoon physics · 6% flourish** — the numbers the brief
   asked for and v1 under-delivered at 3% and 0%.

## Frame direction — the eleven that must be funny

These carry the film. Each is a **recognisable setup with an impossible payoff**,
shot **close**.

| Beat | The joke |
|---|---|
| The argument | Two people mid-argument. Between them, a small workshop is visibly assembling two *completely different* versions of the same living room, and neither notices |
| No file | Protagonist yanks a drawer open with total confidence; it is not a drawer, it comes fully out of the wall, and behind it is just more wall |
| Blue curtains | The protagonist gestures proudly at the curtains — and the curtains are still wet, with a small archivist frozen mid-brushstroke, making eye contact with the viewer |
| The workshop | Reverse angle, close: an archivist eating a sandwich directly over an open folder, crumbs going in |
| Wrong-sized wall | A wall flat two feet too short, propped up with a stack of mugs. Nobody is troubled |
| The PROBABLY box | An archivist reaches into a box without looking and pulls out a completely different person's face |
| Broken glass | Loftus says "smashed" — and the word physically knocks the protagonist's head back like a punch. Red glass sprays into a memory that wasn't there a second ago |
| The family story | Protagonist telling the story with their mouth, in their mum's exact posture, while their mum's hand operates them from behind like a puppet |
| The copy becomes the master | An archivist files the rebuilt folder and, in the same motion, drops the original in the bin without looking |
| Ask for the original | Close on four archivists in a row, all suddenly, deeply interested in the ceiling |
| The last frame | Protagonist opens their mouth to object — and a small hand from off-frame quietly closes a folder over it |
