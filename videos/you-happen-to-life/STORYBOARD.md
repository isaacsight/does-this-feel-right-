## The motif, and its discipline

**THE DIAL** is the only recurring object. It is introduced in Act 2 as the
instrument Rotter built, read in Act 5 as a thermometer rather than a lever,
and turned outward in Act 6 to face a stranger. It appears in **no frame where
it is not doing work**, and nothing else in the film recurs.

The whole argument is in the object: there is only one dial, and where you set
it for yourself is where you have set it for everyone else.

## Two art-direction rules that override any frame below

**The speaker is never a person.** No likeness, no caricature, no recognisable
individual — shot from behind, from the wings, or as flat silhouette against
stage light. The film argues with a belief that millions hold, and the art has
to hold that line as firmly as the script does.

**The stranger in Act 6 is drawn with exactly the same care as the person.**
Same line weight, same dignity, no shabbiness, no pity signals. Act 6 fails
completely if poverty is drawn as a type.
