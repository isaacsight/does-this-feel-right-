## The game

The narrator is calmly, unfailingly correct. The hero denies, deflects and
re-frames, and is contradicted by the picture every single time. It escalates:
mild correction → he's caught out → he's on the floor insisting he's thinking.
**The turn:** at the very end he stops fighting it. "Okay." That's the arc —
the last line stops being an aphorism and becomes a character giving in.

**Straight man rule reversed.** v2's style block said *nobody in frame ever
acknowledges the absurdity*, which removed the baseline and flattened
everything. Here the narrator IS the baseline. The hero is the absurdity.

## Shot mix — "a little bit of everything"

| Type | Share | For | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Literal** | ~50% | anything the viewer must understand | draw the sentence |
| **Cartoon physics** | ~30% | reactions and emotional beats | he melts, deflates, turns to stone |
| **The chair anchor** | ~15% | the three settings, every mention | same person, same chair, three ways |
| **Absurd flourish** | ~5% | rationed — opossum, lizard | never constant |

**Which one:** *can the viewer be wrong about what they're seeing?* If yes, it
must be literal. If the narration has already made the point and they cannot
misread it, that's where the physics goes.
