# CAST — The Stop-and-Chat

Counts are LAW at point of use. No character is a likeness of any real
person; no researcher and no comedian is ever drawn.

## Principals

**THE MAN** — the recurring protagonist: a small slight man with a round face
and a big confident nose, his head bald and smooth except for a single lonely
PAIR of hairs at the very top of his crown — one thin hair kinking left, one
leaning right — a flat VERMILION RED coat with THREE round buttons, thin
stick legs, ABSURDLY oversized shoes. WANT: to get through the day unspoken-
to. His ARC: the gap he defends all film is the gap he crosses at the end.
NEVER smug; his face does dread, calculation, relief, hope.

**THE FAMILIAR STRANGER** — this film's counterpart: a tall composed woman in
a mustard GOLD coat with ONE row of round buttons and ONE small ink-blue
scarf, always carrying ONE completely BLANK folded newspaper. She stands in
the SAME position on the platform in every platform frame. She and the Man
have never spoken; the film is the distance between them.

**THE FIVE REGULARS** — five coated commuters (the Familiar Stranger is one
of the five) who hold the SAME five positions on the platform every time it
recurs: mustard coat, sage coat, ink-blue coat, dusty-orange coat, cream
coat. Formally still, faces composed, never blank.

## Supporting

**THE BARISTA** — a broad warm-faced man in a sage GREEN apron behind ONE
counter with ONE brass machine; the warm act's host. Played straight, full
dignity — the one character who closes distance for a living.
**THE ELEVATOR PAIR** — the Man and ONE other passenger, opposite corners,
both studying the ceiling; ONE panel of round unlit buttons (BLANK).
**THE BENCH** — one long park bench; the Man at one end, ONE seated figure
at the other, the empty middle painted as carefully as either of them.
**CLUB SILHOUETTES** — rows of individuated dark figures for the ONE twist
frame; always populated, never empty black.

## Interaction quota

- The Man and the Familiar Stranger share at least FOUR frames across the
  film without ever facing each other — until the final act, where they
  stand at conversational distance for the first time (staged: both turned,
  gap closed to two paces, mouths CLOSED — the film ends the moment before).
- The avoidance arc appears at least TWICE as footprint geometry.
- Two-figure frames say "exactly TWO figures" at point of use (occupants
  prior). Crowd frames individuate every face (composed, never blank).

## Palette / era

Vermilion = the Man. Mustard = the Familiar Stranger + lamplight. Sage =
the barista + park. Ink blue = platform dusk, elevator, club dark, snow
band. Dusty orange = coffee-shop warmth. The warm act keys warm cream.
