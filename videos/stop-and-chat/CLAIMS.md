| # | Claim | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nine experiments, 800+ Chicago train and bus commuters: asked to talk to a stranger, sit alone, or commute as normal. Being spoken TO also measured positive. | Epley & Schroeder 2014, "Mistakenly Seeking Solitude," J. Experimental Psychology: General | VERIFIED |
| 2 | 932 conversations measured: fewer than 2% ended when both people wanted them to end. About 48% ran too long, about 34% ended too soon. The average gap between desired and actual length was roughly HALF the conversation. | Mastroianni, Gilbert, Cooney & Wilson 2021, PNAS | VERIFIED |
| 3 | The liking gap: after conversations, people consistently underestimate how much the other person liked them. | 4 | People told to genuinely interact with their coffee-shop barista (eye contact, smile, brief chat) measured more positive affect and stronger belonging than people told to be efficient. | 5 | The familiar stranger, 1972: photos taken of a commuter platform, shown back a week later. 89% recognized at least one person.   it does not prescribe a cure. "Measured happier" — never "will fix you."
- Larry David, Jimmy Carr, Epley, Milgram: never named in narration or on
  site copy, never drawn, no frame may resemble any of them (likeness law).

  the-joke law covers the avoider. WE are the avoider.

  generalize to "everyone everywhere."
