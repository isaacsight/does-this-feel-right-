# Why Humans Need Rituals — visual truth

The film takes place in an outdoor world where nature and handmade technology
meet: forest stages, stone doorways, garden laboratories, communal tables,
ceremonial bridges, weather instruments, small robots, animals, and fictional
beings.

The recurring lead has a perfectly round cream face, two black dot eyes, one
expressive eyebrow, a tiny mouth, short antenna-like black hair, a tomato-red
cropped jacket, black trousers, cream sneakers, and a cream cross-body bag.
The lead begins braced and embarrassed, becomes curious, joins other beings,
faces grief and transition, and ends able to cross a threshold. Each reaction
must be readable without narration.

All beings use the same drawing language: bold black brush contours, simple
dot eyes, small mouths, flat spot colors, and precise acting through hands,
posture, silhouette, and eye line. Animals, robots, and fictional creatures
belong to this same world. No photorealism and no second illustration system.

Every generated plate contains exactly five visible colors: black, cream
paper, tomato red, moss green, and cyan. Tomato red is the only accent.

The frame should be understandable in under two seconds. Each plate contains
one action, one emotional reaction, and at most three foreground figures.
Visible hands hold one named object or rest behind a body or prop. No lettering
appears on clothing, screens, signs, packaging, books, architecture, or in the
background.

Every generated prompt follows `../../docs/image-prompt-law.md`: one literal
situation, late-1980s four-ink risograph manga print, physical camera facts,
the exact counted palette, explicit absences, and exactly one print flaw.

The edit uses ninety-six different still images and hard cuts only. There are
no pans, zooms, parallax moves, generated clips, captions, music, or sound
effects.
