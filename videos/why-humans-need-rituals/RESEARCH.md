# Research ledger — Why Humans Need Rituals

## Working definition

For this video, a ritual is a predefined sequence of actions that is repeated,
formalized, or symbolically meaningful while not being fully explained by its
direct practical result. Rituals can be religious or secular, private or
collective. A routine mainly helps complete an instrumental task; a ritual can
also mark a boundary, regulate attention, or locate a person within a group.

## Claims we can make

### Ritualized patterns and uncertainty

- A preregistered experiment with 268 participants compared a predictable,
  repetitive motor or verbal pattern with an unpredictable control and a
  neutral condition after an anxiety induction. The paper tests whether
  redundancy, repetition, and rigidity can modulate anxiety; its findings
  support a qualified relationship rather than a universal cure.
  Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9649661/
- Another preregistered EEG experiment found that an arbitrary repeated ritual
  reduced the error-related neural response after mistakes without producing a
  clear performance improvement or impairment. This supports careful language
  about regulation of failure monitoring, not claims that rituals make the
  brain perform better in every setting.
  Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5452956/
- A 2016 series of experiments reported that actions described as a ritual
  reduced performance anxiety and sometimes improved performance more than the
  same actions described as random behavior. Treat this as one research
  program, not a final law.
  Source: https://hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/Rituals%20OBHDP_5cbc5848-ef4d-4192-a320-68d30169763c.pdf

### Loss and regained control

- Three experiments found lower reported grief after participants reflected
  on personal mourning rituals or completed a novel ritual following a loss.
  Increased feelings of control statistically mediated the relationship. The
  study covered several kinds of loss and should not be presented as a clinical
  treatment for bereavement.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23398180/
- A later scoping review found ritual elements across evidence-informed grief
  interventions, while also showing that the interventions and evidence are
  heterogeneous.
  Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7887294/

### Life transitions

- Arnold van Gennep’s classic framework describes rites of passage as a common
  sequence: separation from an old role, a liminal period between roles, and
  incorporation into a new role. It is an anthropological framework, not a
  neural mechanism.
  Source: https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/R/bo38180827.html

### Synchrony, bonding, and cooperation

- A meta-analysis of 60 published and unpublished experiments found a
  medium-sized association between interpersonal synchrony and prosocial
  attitudes and behavior, with important moderators and contradictory
  individual results.
  Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5137339/
- A field experiment in a stadium found that synchrony and physiological
  arousal predicted tighter clustering and more cooperation in a free-rider
  task; the strongest effects appeared when synchrony and arousal occurred
  together.
  Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5760525/
- A review warns that synchrony has trade-offs: it can support cohesion and
  coordination while also increasing conformity, obedience, group conflict,
  and reduced creativity under some conditions.
  Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7423264/

### Shared emotion

- A 2024 naturalistic study of a public religious ritual examined how physical
  proximity related to aligned autonomic arousal, supporting cautious language
  about emotional contagion in real gatherings.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38838077/
- A preregistered survey after a national funeral found that intense shared
  sadness was associated with identity fusion and pro-group commitment. It was
  observational and cannot establish that the funeral caused every measured
  difference.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39025862/

## Evidence boundaries

- Do not say that rituals “rewire the brain.”
- Do not say rituals always reduce anxiety or improve performance.
- Do not treat synchrony as automatically benevolent.
- Do not confuse a religious, cultural, or therapeutic ritual with a
  compulsive act.
- Do not use one laboratory ritual to explain every historical tradition.
- Present mechanisms as partial explanations: predictability, attention,
  regained control, shared arousal, synchrony, meaning, and social recognition
  can overlap.
- Avoid clinical advice. If a repeated act becomes distressing, compulsory, or
  disruptive, it is not solved by celebrating it as a useful ritual.

## Story implication

The scientific story is strongest when framed as a threshold problem. Humans
often know that a life event has happened before they emotionally know how to
enter what comes next. Ritual gives the transition an order, a witness, and an
ending.
