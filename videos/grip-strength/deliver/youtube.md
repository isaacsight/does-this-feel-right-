## Description

> Squeeze a steel handle as hard as you can. That number predicts when you
> die — in the PURE study of 142,861 people across 17 countries, every five
> missing kilograms of grip meant a 16 percent higher risk of dying, and grip
> predicted mortality better than systolic blood pressure. The UK Biobank
> checked with half a million more people. Same answer.
>
> And the obvious move — train your grip — is the wrong one. This is about
> why the dial is really a receipt printer, and what the receipt itemizes.
>
> [chapters from deliver/chapters.txt]
>
> SOURCES
>
> Leong et al. (2015), "Prognostic value of grip strength," The Lancet
> 386(9990) — the PURE study: 142,861 participants, 17 countries; HR 1.16
> all-cause mortality per 5 kg lower grip; stronger predictor than systolic
> blood pressure in that cohort.
>
> Celis-Morales et al. (2018), BMJ 361:k1651 — UK Biobank, 502,293
> participants; HR 1.20 (women) / 1.16 (men) per 5 kg.
>
> Fain & Weatherford (2016), Journal of Hand Therapy — millennial grip
> norms vs 1985: men 20-24 at 101 lb vs 121 lb right-hand. 237 volunteers —
> a small study; the film says so.
>
> What this piece does NOT claim: that training your grip extends your life.
> Grip is a marker, not a proven cause — both papers say so, and so does the
> film. The grip is the receipt, not the purchase.
