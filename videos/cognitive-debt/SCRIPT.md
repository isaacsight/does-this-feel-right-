# BRIEF — Cognitive Debt

Working title only. **Break the title pattern**: the last three films are
*You Are Not Finished*, *You Happen to Life*, *You Watched It Happen*. A fourth
"You ___" makes the channel page read as a template, which is the same
catalogue risk the Art Director now guards against on the visual side. Find a
title that is not second-person-imperative.

## The angle

Everyone now finishes things with AI and cannot reconstruct how they got there.
Everyone has privately wondered what that costs. One viral preprint told them,
and the discourse is currently one study deep.

## What the film has to do

Not a debunk. A debunk would be dishonest here, because the worry may well be
right — and because **this film is written, boarded and narrated with AI, by a
publication that exists because of it.** The film must retract its own
strongest evidence and then admit the worry survives anyway. That is the
Director's own rule about the honest beat, arriving in its hardest form: the
evidence being retracted is the evidence that flatters our thesis.

## The material, unverified — audit every line of this yourself

**The study that named it.** Kosmyna et al., MIT Media Lab, *Your Brain on
ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay
Writing Task*. EEG across three groups (brain-only / search engine / LLM);
brain-only showed the strongest, most distributed connectivity, LLM users the
weakest. Went massively viral; "cognitive debt" entered the culture as a fact.

**Turn one — the study cannot carry what was hung on it.** arXiv preprint, ~54
participants, and only ~18 completed the session the headline rests on. Not
peer-reviewed at the time of publication. The authors themselves wrote that
conclusions should be treated as preliminary. A 2026 comment (Stanković et al.)
flagged small-n, EEG methodology and reproducibility.

**Turn two — this has happened before, and that is the film.** Sparrow, Liu &
Wegner, *Google Effects on Memory*, Science, 2011: search engines change what
you bother to remember. It became the defining fact about technology and the
mind for a decade. The "Google Stroop" component **failed to replicate** in the
major 2018 attempt (Social Sciences Replication Project / Camerer et al.).
Every technology gets its study. The studies keep not surviving. The belief
survives anyway.

**The honest beat.** The offloading findings — remembering *where* rather than
*what* — have held up better than the flashy priming ones. So the direction may
be right even though the headline evidence is thin. Find the version of this
that does not let the viewer, or us, off the hook.

**Candidate landing.** The thing worth watching is probably not whether you
remember, but whether you can still *reconstruct the reasoning* — the
difference between having an answer and being able to rebuild it. Test that
against the evidence; if it does not survive the audit, find one that does.
