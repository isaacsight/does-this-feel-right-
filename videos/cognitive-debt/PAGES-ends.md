# PAGES — THE TWO ENDS

> Writer's chair. Filed 2026-08-04 against `TREATMENT.md` (locked) and
> `script.txt`. Two stretches only: Act 1 entire, and the last four sentences of
> Act 5. Nothing else in the film is touched, proposed, or reordered.

---

## Angle

Both ends of this film are currently gestures. Act 1 names an experience instead
of inhabiting one, and the close asks the viewer to "walk it back" without ever
saying what walking it back would look like at a kitchen table on a Tuesday
night. My assignment is to put a room, an hour, and an object at the front, and
to put a doable, checkable, unpromising test at the back — and to make the
"step you did not take" motif land as a *picture* on both ends, since it is
about to be drawn four or five times.

Constraint I held throughout: the close must be a test, not a prescription. The
narrator reports what the test consists of and what it does not tell you. It
never says the viewer should run it, and it never says what running it will do
for them. Anything that promises an outcome is the film selling the exact
comfort it just took away.

## Lines I would fight for

1. **"You can point at the sentence before it. You can point at the sentence
   after it. The part between them arrived."** This is the motif made drawable.
   Two things you can indicate and one you cannot — that is a frame, and it is
   the same frame every time it recurs. The current script's *"somewhere in the
   middle of it there was a step that you did not take"* is a sentence; this is
   an image with a hole in it.

2. **"Not wrong. Just not yours the way the rest of it is yours."** Eight words
   and five words. It is the whole discomfort of the film without the word
   "debt", and it is the reason the viewer stays for the study.

3. **"The test is not whether the answer is right. It is whether a reason comes
   up at all, or only the shape of one."** This is the close doing its job
   without counselling. It states the condition and withholds the verdict. Note
   there is no "you should", no benefit, and no second half of the sentence
   telling anyone what to feel about the result.

---

## Lines I am unsure of

- **"It took about ten minutes."** A film that spends four minutes on how a
  number got repeated without its context should probably not open with a number
  it made up. It earns its place — the speed *is* the recognition — but if the
  Director wants it gone, the replacement that keeps the beat is: *"It took less
  time than it used to."* Six words, no invention, slightly softer landing.

- **"It was better than you are on a Tuesday."** My favourite line in the pages
  and therefore the one I trust least. It is warm, and Act 1 is meant to be
  cool; the treatment reserves the film's only warm patch for Act 5. If it reads
  as the narrator being fond of the viewer, cut it and let *"better than fine"*
  stand alone. The scene survives.

- **"Open the last thing you sent with your name at the top of it."** This is an
  imperative, and imperatives are one step from advice. I believe it stays on
  the right side of the line because it describes the test rather than
  recommending it — and because the locked script already carried *"Take the
  thing you finished this week."* But it is the seam, and if the close ever
  starts to feel like a lifehack, this is where it went wrong.

- **"say out loud, to nobody"** is doing two jobs: it makes the test physical
  and it rhymes with Act 1's *"never said it out loud."* If Act 1's version is
  cut for length, this one loses half its weight and should probably become
  *"say it out loud"*.

## Structural notes

Written as notes. Nothing below is enacted in the pages.

1. **The register margins are thinner after this, not looser.** The treatment
   records `you` at 37.8 against a floor of 35 and `we` at 21.3 against a
   ceiling of 22. Both of my stretches are second-person-heavy, which pushes
   `you` up (harmless, the ceiling is 58) and dilutes `we` per thousand
   (roughly 21.3 → 17-18 at 978 words). That is still inside the 8–22 band, but
   it moves the film *away* from the confession the treatment deliberately
   bought. The Act 5 warm patch — *"We cannot get back inside it either"* — is
   now carrying more of the `we` load than it was. **Re-run
   `register-profile.py --gate` before boarding; do not assume.**

2. **The remaining forty seconds should not come from the ends.** The brief has
   room for ~90 more seconds and these pages spend 46. My read is that Act 3 is
   where the film is currently thinnest against its own ambition: the decay from
   five-in-six to six-of-eighteen is the thesis as a single picture, and it goes
   past in two sentences. That is a director's call and another writer's page. I
   am flagging it, not writing it.

3. **The callback now has three surfaces, not two.** Act 1 establishes *the part
   between them arrived*; Act 5 says *the step in the middle that you did not
   take — the part that arrived*. If the Art Director wants a third instance for
   the recurring image, the natural slot is Act 2 at *"We have all sent
   something we could not quote back an hour later"* — but I have not written
   one, because a motif that appears in every act is not a motif, it is a
   refrain, and this film is too cool for a refrain.

4. **The cold open has no title card in it and should not get one.** The film's
   first spoken word is now "It", and the strongest version of this opening is
   the one where the viewer is inside the kitchen before they know what the film
   is about. If a title needs to land, it lands on *"Last June, a paper told
   us."*
