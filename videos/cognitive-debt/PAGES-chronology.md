## Angle

Make Act 4's landing line a documented fact instead of a claim. The belief that
machines are costing us something cognitively is datable, and it is older than
the study that got taken as its proof. Nicholas Carr's essay ran in the summer of
2008. Sparrow, Liu and Wegner published three years later. The film currently
asserts *we had the feeling first*; my job is to put a date on the feeling and a
date on the footnote, and let the gap do the work.

The find that changed the draft: **Sparrow et al. cite Carr.** Reference 9 of the
Science paper is `N. Carr, Atlantic 302, 56 (2008)`, and it appears exactly once,
inside a hedge — *"the disadvantages of being constantly 'wired' are still being
debated (9)."* The study everyone read as the answer to Carr filed Carr under
open question. The film's word *footnote* stops being a metaphor.

## Pages

**Insert in ACT 4, immediately before the existing final line.** Runs after
*"Nobody came to the funeral. The belief carried on without it, which tells you
what that study was really doing for us. It was not helping us find out, it was
helping us confirm."* — and hands straight to *"We had the feeling first, and then
we went looking for a footnote."*

> The study did not start the belief either. Three years before it, in the summer
> of 2008, The Atlantic ran an essay by Nicholas Carr called Is Google Making Us
> Stupid. You have met that title. It was an essay about attention, and it did not
> claim to be evidence. Anecdotes alone don't prove much, he wrote, and we still
> await the long-term neurological and psychological experiments. Three years
> later, one arrived. It cites Carr once, in a sentence saying that the
> disadvantages of being constantly wired are still being debated. The worry was
> in print first. The evidence turned up second, and got read as the answer.

### On the third case — I am not proposing one

The brief asked whether a documented third case makes this a pattern. I looked,
and my recommendation is **no, not in this act, not at this budget.**

The nearest clean candidate is television and children's attention. It is real:
Christakis, Zimmerman, DiGiuseppe & McCarty, *Early television exposure and
subsequent attentional problems in children*, **Pediatrics** 113(4), 708–713
(2004) — *"Early television exposure is associated with attentional problems at
age 7"* (opened, PubMed 15060216). Six years later Foster & Watkins, *The Value
of Reanalysis: TV Viewing and Attention Problems*, **Child Development** 81(1),
368–375 (2010), reanalysed the same NLSY data and concluded *"The earlier
findings are not robust… no meaningful relation between television viewing and
attention problems."* That second one I have from the journal listing and
abstract text, **not from the full paper — mark it VERIFIED (secondary)** if it
is ever narrated.

Three reasons to leave it out. It costs sixty words the act does not have. Its
"feeling first" anchor is weaker — I can date the study and the failure, but the
pre-existing worry would rest on Marie Winn's *The Plug-In Drug* (1977), which I
did not open. And structurally, three cases turn a specific, documented
observation into a survey. The Carr chronology is stronger *because* it is
narrow: one essay, one paper, one footnote, three years. A pattern claim the film
can only half-show would be the film doing the thing the film is about.

If the Director wants the third case anyway, ask for it as its own assignment
with its own budget. It should not be a clause.

## Lines I would fight for

1. **"It cites Carr once, in a sentence saying that the disadvantages of being
   constantly wired are still being debated."** This is the whole angle in one
   sentence and it is checkable to the line number. It also converts the film's
   own closing word — *footnote* — from figure of speech to literal object. If
   only one sentence of mine survives, this one.
2. **"Anecdotes alone don't prove much, he wrote, and we still await the
   long-term neurological and psychological experiments."** Carr disclaiming his
   own essay, in his own words, is the film's fairness on the record. It stops
   Act 4 from making Carr the villain, which he is not — he was precise, and we
   were not.
3. **"The worry was in print first."** Six words. It is the act's thesis and it
   is a date, not an opinion.

## Lines I am unsure of

- **"You have met that title."** It is doing register work as much as argument
  work, and it repeats Act 3's move (*"You probably met that phrase before you
  ever met the study"*). That echo is either the best thing in the insert — the
  same failure, seventeen years apart, phrase outrunning paper — or it is the
  film saying the same thing twice. I lean deliberate. The Director should decide
  whether the rhyme reads as design.
- **"and got read as the answer."** Sourced as reception, not as text. See the
  source table. I would cut it before I would defend it.
- **"The study did not start the belief either."** The "either" is load-bearing
  and depends entirely on this block following *"Nobody came to the funeral"* and
  the *carried on without it* pair. If the Director moves the insert earlier in
  Act 4 — say, before the 2018 and 2020 failures — the word breaks and the line
  becomes *"The study did not start the belief."* Flagging because it is the kind
  of thing a reorder silently ruins.

## Structural notes

**Register arithmetic, which the Director must run before this ships.** The
locked script is 842 words at 37.8 `you` per thousand — about 32 instances —
against a floor of 35. My insert is 108 words containing **one** `you` and one
`we` (Carr's, inside the quote). Adding it alone puts the script at roughly
33.5 `you` per thousand, **below the floor.** The optional swap above lifts the
insert to three `you` and gets the whole script to roughly 35.4 — clearing, but
by less than one instance. If other writers are filing `you`-light material into
the same rebuild, this insert cannot be the one that carries the ratio. Run
`register-profile.py --gate` on the merged script, not on any one insert.

**On the ninety seconds.** This is 37 of them and it is the last argument Act 4
has. I do not think Act 4 should take the rest. The act's coldest moment is
*"Nobody came to the funeral"* and it wants air after it, per the treatment's own
intent note; if the missing minute all lands here, that silence gets crowded out
by facts. My reading is that Act 4 takes this block and no more, and the
remaining time belongs to Acts 1 and 5 — where the film is about us rather than
about papers, and where extra room reads as thinking rather than as evidence.
That is a note, not an edit.

**Order inside the act is unchanged.** The insert is additive and sits between
two existing sentences. No line of the locked script is moved, cut, or reworded
by this filing.

**One thing recorded and not narrated, per the treatment's own precedent.** The
Sparrow paper's sentence immediately after the Carr citation reads: *"It may be
no more that nostalgia at this point, however, to wish we were less dependent on
our gadgets."* (The typo is the paper's.) It is a gift of a line and I left it
out. Narrating it would let the film use the authors to sneer at the worry, and
the register forbids that as firmly as it forbids sneering at the study. Filed
for the colophon.
