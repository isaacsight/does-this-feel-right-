# PAGES — the complaint is older than the machine

Writer's chair. Filed 2026-08-04 for the new act between Act 3 (the retraction)
and Act 4 (the history/turn).

## Angle

Why we believed it so fast: because we already believed it, and had for
twenty-four centuries. The worry that a tool is eating our minds is not about
AI and never rested on evidence — the oldest surviving statement of it is a
king in an Egyptian folk tale inside a Platonic dialogue, refusing writing.
The act's job is to show the viewer that the belief did not come *from* the
study, which is why breaking the study will not remove it.

The act does **not** land on "people always panic and they are always wrong."
That is a tech-industry talking point, it is false, and it contradicts Act 5.
It lands on: the complaint has been partly right the whole time, and it was
never a measurement — so no measurement can end it, and we keep buying them
anyway.

## Pages

### NEW ACT — THE COMPLAINT IS OLDER THAN THE MACHINE

The worry did not start with the machine, and it did not start with a study. It
did not start with you either.

In the fourth century BC, Plato wrote a dialogue called the Phaedrus. Near the
end of it, Socrates tells a story about Egypt. A god named Theuth brings his
inventions to King Thamus, and the great one is writing. It will make the
Egyptians wiser, he says. An elixir of memory.

The king says no. Writing, he says, will create forgetfulness in the learners'
souls, because they will not use their memories. They will trust the marks
outside them and stop remembering from within. They will have the show of
wisdom without the reality.

If you have ever repeated a fact you could not have reached on your own, he
means you.

There is no measurement anywhere in it. No sample, no control, no number. A
king had an intuition, and it has outlived every study anyone has run about it
since.

And he was not simply wrong. Something did get handed over. You are trusting
the marks right now to hold things you have stopped holding, and mostly you
could not say which things they are.

Socrates wrote nothing himself. The only reason you have ever heard his
objection to writing is that somebody wrote it down. It did not slow writing
down by a day.

A worry that arrives twenty-four centuries ahead of its evidence was never
resting on evidence. Which is why no study can settle it. And why we keep
buying the ones that try.

### Left out on purpose

- **Print, television, calculators, search.** The brief allowed a second
  example and the other writer's finding on the television case is the reason
  there is none here: an unsourced list of moral panics *is* the tech-industry
  talking point this act is written against, and one properly sourced modern
  example would cost 40 words the act does not have. Plato alone carries it,
  because the point is the *distance*, not the count.
- **Nicholas Carr, *Is Google Making Us Stupid?* (The Atlantic, July/August
  2008).** Already earmarked in the treatment as Act 4 material — the worry
  predating Sparrow 2011 by three years. Putting it here would spend Act 4's
  best chronological beat one act early. Flagged so it is not double-spent.
- **Any claim that literacy measurably changed human memory.** The honest turn
  is made without it, because I could not open a source I trusted for it in the
  time available, and the act's argument does not need one: "he was not simply
  wrong" is a judgement the film is entitled to make and does not need to
  measure. Making it measured would be the exact error the film is about.

## Lines I would fight for

1. **"There is no measurement anywhere in it. No sample, no control, no number. A king had an intuition, and it has outlived every study anyone has run about it since."**
   This is the act's whole job in three sentences, and it is the sentence that
   makes Act 3's collapse survivable — the belief was never load-bearing on
   evidence, so the evidence falling does not move it.
2. **"Socrates wrote nothing himself. The only reason you have ever heard his objection to writing is that somebody wrote it down."**
   True, checkable, and it does the un-smug work: the complaint was preserved
   *by* the thing it complained about. It refuses both cheap endings at once.
3. **"A worry that arrives twenty-four centuries ahead of its evidence was never resting on evidence. Which is why no study can settle it. And why we keep buying the ones that try."**
   The exit condition, stated. The last clause is the one `we` in the act and
   it is where the act hands over to the turn.

## Lines I am unsure of

- **"It did not start with you either."** Added late, partly to carry the `you`
  floor. It scans, but it may be answering a question the viewer has not asked
  yet — nothing before this act suggested the worry *did* start with them. The
  act survives without it at 252 words and a `you` rate of 31.5/1k, which is
  under the floor share. Director's call on which cost is cheaper.
- **"It did not slow writing down by a day."** My favourite sound in the act
  and the least sourced thing in it. Also the "down … down" of "slow writing
  down" sits badly next to "wrote it down" in the sentence before. If it stays,
  it may want to be "It did not slow writing by a day."
- **"he means you."** Three words doing a lot of pointing. In a film that
  deliberately sits at the `you` floor to avoid accusing a viewer it has no
  standing to accuse, this is the most accusatory line I wrote. It is also the
  moment a 2,400-year-old passage stops being a curio. I think it earns its
  keep; I am not certain it is in register.
