## 1. The world, and what is true of every frame

The world is **locked and tested** at `WORLD.md`. This board does not vary it and
does not rediscover it. Every prompt carries the locked ledger clause verbatim —
the doubled colour phrasing is load-bearing and is never shortened — plus the
house common block.

Plates installed: `production/refs/castplate.png` (protagonist, three views,
ledger-native) and `production/refs/layout-plate.png` (the empty wide).

**The protagonist**, described at point of use in every frame that contains them
(PLAYBOOK 10.20 — a reference sheet is necessary and not sufficient): *a small
round-headed figure, head large in proportion to the body, with **exactly two**
fine hairs standing up from the crown, a plain tomato-red hooded jacket, black
trousers, pale cream shoes. Two short dark brows, two dot eyes, a small nose, no
mouth unless the beat needs one.*

### The three prohibitions this film is most exposed to

Each is enforced by **deleting the object**, never by adding a rule — a
prohibition loses to a description:

1. **No marks on anything.** The narration says *a Mac*, *ChatGPT*, *The
   Atlantic*, *Science*, *an SAT paper*. **No object in this film carries a
   logo, a badge, a title, a screen image, a numeral or a printed line.** A
   writing machine is *a plain hinged slab with a blank pale panel and a key
   deck, no marks on it*. A journal is *a thick loose stack of blank pale
   sheets*. The world clause already forbids writing on the ruled ground; this
   extends it to every object standing on it.
2. **Nicholas Carr is a living person and is never a portrait.** He is boarded as
   *an adult figure seen from behind and slightly to one side so the face is
   never visible, seated in a plain chair.* Face never shown in any Act 4 frame.
   Safer, and better cinema.
3. **The narrator has no body.** No figure addresses camera, points at the
   viewer, or holds a pointer. The protagonist is the person the film is *about*,
   never its presenter.

### Two things happen. Never five.

Every scene below is **one dominant event in the foreground, at most one small
callback behind it, and otherwise empty ruled ground.** Where a detail did not
serve the one event it was cut from the scene text rather than demoted.

### The wide audit

Chair limit: **three wide establishing shots.** This film uses exactly three —
`b01` (the cold open), `b33` (the room emptying), `b88` (the closing image).

Separately, this world's tested affordance — *on ruled ground an empty frame
reads as paper, not as void* — is used in **nine field compositions**: `b06`,
`b11`, `b12`, `b31`, `b57`, `b65`, `b69`, `b81`, `b83`. These are not
establishing shots: each is a small figure in a large ruled field **with a
specific foreground event**, which is the distinction, and they are listed here
so QC can check it rather than take it on trust.

### Motif 1 · THE STAIR — the film's principal motif, ONE object

**What the step IS as a picture.** Act 1's *"a step that you did not take"* and
Act 6's *"the step in the middle that you did not take, the part that arrived"*
are the same object, drawn the same way, from a single master plate:

> **A free-standing flight of exactly seven treads, alone on the ruled ground,
> rising left to right, with no handrail, no risers, no wall, no building and no
> doorway at the top — the top tread simply stops in the air. Treads are
> near-black outline with pale sage flat fill. The fourth tread — the middle one,
> with three below it and three above it — is tomato-red and sits a hair proud of
> the line of the others.**

Why this and not a page: it is wordless (the world forbids lettering), it is
countable, it holds a gap without ambiguity, and Act 1's language is literally
*point at the one below, point at the one above*. The gate question — *can the
viewer be wrong about what they are seeing?* — answers no.

**It has two states and the script requires both.** In Act 1 the step is
*missing*; then it **arrives**, and it is *not yours* — the tread is present and
it is a different colour from every other tread. That is the whole film in one
object, and it is why the motif is a stair and not a hole.

| Plate | State | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| `refs/stair.png` | **MASTER.** Seven treads, the fourth tomato-red. | anchor for every stair frame |
| `refs/stair-gap.png` | The fourth tread simply absent — clean air between tread three and tread five, everything else identical. Generated **from the master as reference #1**. Doubles as production frame `b06`. | `b06` |

**State what it is NOT**, in the plate prompt: *not a staircase in a building,
not attached to a wall, no handrail, no banister, no landing, no door, no
ladder, no steps carved into ground.* Every generic prior for "stairs" summons an
interior.

**Anchor, do not chain.** `b06`, `b07`, `b08`, `b83`, `b85`, `b87` all take
`stair.png` as reference #1, ahead of the castplate. Group invariants (named in
every scene text, because a generic "locked camera" clause has failed on its
own): the bottom tread meets the same ruled line as the figure's feet; tread
depth and rise identical across all seven; the flight occupies the same fraction
of frame width; the red tread is always fourth from the bottom.

### Motif 4 · THE FOOTNOTE — the plant and the payoff, one composition seen twice

The Director verified that Carr's essay is **reference 9 in Sparrow 2011, the
last entry in the printed list, cited exactly once**. The film's closing word,
*footnote*, is that footnote. This board takes the rhyme.

| Plate | What it is |
|---|---|
| `refs/footnote.png` | Derived from `sheaf.png`. Close, from above: the **last sheet of the stack lifted at its bottom corner** by the protagonist's hand, and **one small tomato-red dot at the very bottom edge of that sheet, under the fingertip. The plant is `b53` (Act 4, 3:5x) and the payoff is `b72` (Act 5's landing line,

viewer does not need to be told they are the same shot; they need to not be able
to miss it.

### Motif 5 · THE SHELF — Act 6's double object

Act 6 needs *"a gain and a cost in the same object, not two objects."*

| Plate | What it is |
|---|---|
| `refs/shelf.png` | A single plain bracketed shelf board high on the ruled ground, one plain pale box on it. The protagonist beneath with **both hands empty and open**, one arm raised, fingertips short of the board. |

One object. The gain (empty hands) and the cost (out of reach) are the same
picture, at the same instant. Used by `b75`, `b76`, `b77`, `b78`.

### Not a motif — declared so QC does not read it as drift · THE GROUND LINE

The single near-black horizon line is a **world element**, present in
`layout-plate.png`. It is used as an *active* element in exactly two beats —
`b11` (the line continues ahead of the walker on its own) and `b81` (the walker
goes back along it) — because those two lines are a callback pair the script sets
up and pays off. Both anchor to `layout-plate.png`. It is a subject nowhere else.

### Objects deliberately given NO plate

The sand timer (2 beats), the four commenters (2 beats), the plinth row (3 beats,
governed by group G3b), the crowd field (3 beats, governed by group G3). Each is
under the three-beat threshold or is a *composition* rather than an object, which
a group handles better than a plate.

## 4. Motion — the marks, and the five LIVE beats

`STILL` is the default and 81 of 88 beats take it. `DRIFT` is the composition's
free deterministic move and is used on two beats, as an inverse pair.

**Five `LIVE` beats, never adjacent, none carrying data.** 28 seconds of clip at
$0.04/s = **$1.12**.

**Craft constraints, in every clip brief:** one motion only; motion begins *after*
the cut lands so the composition registers first and the life in it second; slow
enough to watch; ends on a settle, never on a visible loop.

**Every LIVE clip is generated image-to-video from the approved still, never
text-to-video.** Not stylistic: L1's stair has seven treads and L4's sheet has one
dot, and a text-to-video pass would re-derive that geometry and break the motif.

### Beats deliberately refused for LIVE — recorded so it is not re-litigated

- **`b69` — *"Nobody came to the funeral."*** The coldest line in the film. The
  treatment asks for stillness and space and it is right; a clip here would be
  motion as apology dressed as gravity. **STILL, and long.**
- **`b21` `b31` `b33` `b34` `b37` `b66`.** Every one carries a count, a comparison
  or a proportion — the geometry *is* the argument. **Never LIVE** (PLAYBOOK
  10.17b: our own chart came back as roughly eight bars of equal height,
  contradicting its own narration).
- **`b26` — *"Cognitive debt."*** The act's landing click. A click is a cut, not
  a move.

3. **`b19` places one tomato-red arrowhead on a cord.** Same reasoning, same
