# CLAIMS — Somebody Has to Die Now (the duel)

Every factual claim in the script, with its source. Verified 2026-08-05.

1. **The code duello was written in 1777 at the Clonmel Summer Assizes,
   Ireland**, by gentlemen of Tipperary, Galway, Mayo, Sligo and Roscommon,
   "prescribed for general adoption throughout Ireland," and adopted far
   beyond it (notably in America).
   ~26 rules (sources vary 25/26).
   - PBS American Experience, "Code Duello: The Rules of Dueling"
   - Oxford Reference, "Irish Code Duello"

2. **A blow could not be apologised for in words.** Code duello: "As a blow
   is strictly prohibited under any circumstances among Gentlemen, no verbal
   apology can be received for such an insult." (Rule 5.)
   - PBS American Experience, code text.

3. **The dog duel, Hyde Park 1803.** Colonel Montgomery (Army) and Captain
   MacNamara (Navy) duelled over which of their two Newfoundland dogs started
   a fight. Montgomery died.
   - militaryhistorynow.com; strangehistory.net (Beachcombing).

4. **The hat duel.** Popoff knocked over Ponikwicki's hat; Ponikwicki called
   him an idiot; the duel killed Popoff on the first shot.
   - strangehistory.net, "The Ten Stupidest Duels in History".

5. **The wine-glass duel.** Captain Smith challenged General Barry after Barry,
   seasick on the Irish Sea crossing, declined his offer of wine.
   - militaryhistorynow.com.

6. **Duels were fought over whether a parrot understood what it spoke** and
   whether a horse could be trained in three days.
   - militaryhistorynow.com.

7. **US Navy, 1798 to the Civil War: dueling killed two-thirds as many
   officers as combat at sea did.**
   - Smithsonian Magazine, "Duel!" (Ross Drake).

8. **Wellington, sitting Prime Minister, fought a duel in 1829** against the
   Earl of Winchilsea (over Catholic emancipation). Both deliberately missed
   or shot wide; the press and cartoonists made it look ridiculous.
   - Appiah, The Honor Code (2010); NYRB review (David Brion Davis).

9. **Dueling in England died within roughly a quarter-century of 1829**, not
   from laws or sermons (both had failed for two centuries) but because the
   definition of a gentleman changed — Newman's gentleman "never inflicts
   pain" — and duelling became ridiculous; the last recorded English duel
   (early 1850s, a merchant and a banker) provoked national ridicule.
   - Appiah, The Honor Code; NYRB; Slate review.

10. **The last French duel was 1967**: Gaston Defferre (mayor of Marseille)
    vs Rene Ribiere, with epees, after Defferre shouted "Shut up, stupid!"
    in the National Assembly. Ribiere was cut twice; nobody died; there is
    film of it.
    - Open Culture; Rare Historical Photos; Granger archive.

## Register note

The pen for this film is a comedian in the Larry David key: the grievance
escalated to principle, the litigation of unwritten rules, the man trapped by
etiquette. The comedy is in the TRUE material — nothing is invented; the jokes
are placement and emphasis, never fabricated facts.
