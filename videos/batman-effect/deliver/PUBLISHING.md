**Description**

> Researchers in Milan wanted to know whether an unexpected event could make
> people kinder. So they put a woman with a prosthetic pregnancy belly on a
> crowded metro train, recorded whether anyone offered her a seat — and then
> did it again with Batman standing three metres away.
>
> He didn't speak. He didn't help. He just stood there, being Batman on public
> transportation.
>
> Seat-offering went from about 38% to about 67%. And roughly 44% of the people
> who gave up their seat said they hadn't noticed him at all.
>
> This video covers what happened, what the researchers think it means, and the
> part most coverage skips: they can't yet tell whether the effect came from
> surprise, from attention, or from seeing a hero associated with helping. One
> clever field study is evidence worth exploring — not a law of human nature.
>
> ── SOURCE ──
> Unexpected events and prosocial behavior: the Batman effect
> npj Mental Health Research, vol. 4 (2025)
> DOI: 10.1038/s44184-025-00171-5
> https://www.nature.com/articles/s44184-025-00171-5
>
> Quasi-experimental field study on the Milan metro. Seat-offering 37.66%
> without Batman, 67.21% with, odds ratio 3.393 (p < 0.001). The authors are
> explicit that the mechanism is not yet separable.
