# CLAIMS — Nobody Roots for Goliath

Every factual claim in the script, with its source. Verified 2026-08-07.

1. **"Nobody roots for Goliath."** Wilt Chamberlain's own line, widely
   attributed and repeated in his obituaries and interviews. The fuller
   version he used: "Everyone roots for David, nobody pulls for Goliath."
   - Chamberlain, quoted widely; NPR tribute (1999).

2. **The underdog effect is real and replicates across domains.** Participants
   showed more rooting, sympathy and identification with struggling entities
   than with non-struggling ones, across athletic, business and artistic
   competitions.
   - Vandello, Goldschmied & Richards (2007), "The Appeal of the Underdog,"
     *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin* 33(12).

3. **Sympathy drives it more than schadenfreude.** People rate their desire for
   the underdog to WIN higher than their desire for the top dog to LOSE — so
   it is not primarily spite.
   - Vandello, Goldschmied & Richards (2007).

4. **We adopt the emotional perspective of the low-power party.** People are
   inclined to take the perspective of powerless rather than powerful others.
   The powerful are expected to feel socially DISENGAGING emotions (contempt,
   anger, pride); the powerless socially ENGAGING ones (gratitude, guilt,
   embarrassment) which maintain relationships.
   - "Sympathy for the underdog: people are inclined to adopt the emotional
     perspective of powerless (versus powerful) others" (2021).

5. **We root for the underdog and then abandon them.** Support is conditional
   and withdraws once the underdog is no longer disadvantaged.
   - Kim et al. (2008), "Rooting for (and Then Abandoning) the Underdog,"
     *Journal of Applied Social Psychology* 38(10).

6. **The support flips when interests are at stake.** Underdog support is
   cheap: it holds on a narrative level, when there is no cost to it.
   Power state changes the appeal — low-power people respond better to
   TOP-DOG appeals (associating with winners restores power), high-power
   people to underdog appeals (supporting underdogs expresses power).
   - Jin & Huang (2019), "How Power States Influence the Persuasiveness of
     Top-Dog versus Underdog Appeals," *Journal of Consumer Psychology*.
   - Rob Henderson, "Nobody Roots for Goliath (Unless Their Interests Are at
     Stake)" — the framing this film takes its turn from.

## What this film does NOT claim

It does not claim betting studies show a specific flip rate — the "money on
it" beat is stated as the mechanism's logic (support is cheap until it costs),
which is what the power-state and narrative-level findings support. Do not
board a fake statistic for it.

It does not claim Chamberlain was measured in any study. He is the opening
anecdote and the film says so.
