# CAST & ENVIRONMENTS — The Million-Dollar Pigeon

Why this file exists: the queue episode was rejected twice, and the note that
fixed it was "there should be interaction with different characters and
environments and landscapes." The board quotas enforce that mechanically, but
quotas can only check that SOMEBODY is in frame — this file is what makes it
the SAME somebodies, wanting things, in places that keep changing. The episode
lives in the interactions, so every character below is defined by what they DO
to somebody else, never by how they look standing still.

Standing law (paid for on the underdog film): a cast file is NOT sufficient by
itself — the board must re-describe each character AT POINT OF USE, with counts
of countable features, every time it names them.

## The two-sided cast

The comedy engine of this episode: every scene has a HUMAN side that means
everything and a BIRD side that means nothing. Where possible, stage both in
the same frame — the auctioneer sweating over a bird that is preening; the
fancier weeping at the sky while the dot above him is just a bird thinking
about his wife.

### THE FANCIER — our recurring man, thirty years on

The series character (small slight man, round face, big confident nose,
EXACTLY TWO kinked hairs, flat VERMILION RED coat, absurdly oversized shoes)
— but here he is the obsessive, not the wronged party. He has a rooftop loft,
a stopwatch on a string around his neck, and a thermos. His want is tiny and
absolute: he wants his bird home FIRST, once, before he dies.

Interactions that define him:
- listens to the loft with a stethoscope while his dinner goes cold below
- polishes a rubber leg-ring like it is a wedding band
- stands in the yard staring at an empty sky with the stopwatch held up,
  hours, through rain, while neighbours pass behind him with umbrellas
- carries the basket to the club van like a father surrendering a child

### THE BIRD — "Kim", the co-lead

A hen drawn with a strong readable silhouette: plump chest, tiny head, ONE
white splash on the left wing (countable, name it at point of use). Her entire
inner life is one thought — going home — and every frame with her should show
that thought while humans project everything else onto her.

Interactions that define her:
- stands perfectly still, mid-preen, while chaos happens around her price
- looks at a medal, then eats a seed off it
- in the basket among DOZENS of other birds, all facing different directions,
  she alone faces the door

### THE BIRD'S HUSBAND — the widower

The engine of the sport and of the third act: a cock in the home loft, waiting.
Design: slightly scruffy, feathers ruffled on one side. He does not know he is
the motivation system of a global industry.

Interactions: paces the loft ledge like an expectant father outside a delivery
room; presses one eye to a gap in the loft slats; when she lands, the two of
them do the most undramatic thing in the world — sit together on a perch —
while the human below erupts.

### THE RIVAL — the fancier across the road

Same age, better equipment. A gleaming aluminium loft with an aerial. Never a
villain: his crime is owning a slightly nicer ladder. All rivalry is conducted
by the two men adjusting their respective rooftop equipment SLIGHTLY too
vigorously while pretending not to look at each other.

### THE AUCTIONEER

Sweating in a sharp suit, gavel in hand, eyes bulging at a screen where the
number climbs — while the lot itself, the bird, preens in a cage beside him.
The two bidders exist only as PADDLES or SCREENS labelled blank (no text law):
one vast and gold, one small and black — Super Duper and Hitman rendered as
objects, never as people.

### THE LAB OFFICIAL — South Africa

A horse-racing man in a white coat, built like a jockey's opposite, holding a
tiny specimen jar up to the light with maximal ceremony, surrounded by
paintings and trophies of HORSES that make the pigeon dropping in his tweezers
look even smaller. His face does the episode's finest disbelief.

### THE SOLDIER — 1918, played straight

Mud-caked, young, hands cupped around the last bird like a match in wind. This
sequence gets NO comedy staging: the Cher Ami act works because the film means
it. The bird is small; the shellfire is enormous; the frames are the quietest
in the episode.

### DARWIN

Beard, loft coat over Victorian dress, notebook. Staged between the two clubs:
one door with top hats through it, one door with flat caps, Darwin cheerfully
in the middle holding a pigeon that ignores the class system entirely.

### THE CHAMPION (Tyson beat) — an original boxer, not a likeness

An ENORMOUS heavyweight in a dressing gown with tiny reading glasses, holding
a pigeon in two cupped gloves like a soap bubble. NO real-person likeness (no
face tattoo, no Tyson trade dress) — the narration carries the fact; the frame
carries only "the most frightening man alive, being gentle."

## Environments — the episode travels

Rule from the queue rebuild: queue-equivalent scenes (loft rooftops) capped;
the film must keep moving. Target no environment above ~30% of frames.

1. **The rooftop loft, Belgium** — home base. Brick chimneys, washing lines,
   a wooden loft with a landing board. Dawn, noon, rain, dusk variants.
2. **The village street below** — neighbours, the rival's roof opposite,
   a church clock with a BLANK face.
3. **The club, basket night** — a back room of men and baskets, sealed clocks
   on a trestle table, one bare bulb.
4. **The liberation** — a VAST field at dawn, one lorry, a THOUSAND birds
   erupting upward in a single spiral. The episode's widest image.
5. **The open sky** — a single dot in an enormous cloudscape; the loneliest
   frames in the film, used for the "invisible sport" act.
6. **The auction hall** — velvet, brass, a cage on a plinth, the climbing
   number rendered as a BLANK gold board with the auctioneer reacting to it.
7. **The South African lab** — horse paintings, horse trophies, one tiny jar.
8. **The Argonne, 1918** — mud, wire, smoke, one bird against it. Desaturated
   toward the world palette's ink blue and cream.
9. **Victorian London** — two club doorways, gas lamps, Darwin's garden loft.
10. **The champion's dressing room** — lockers, a heavy bag, one small cage
    with the door open.

## Interaction quota (feeds the board)

Every act must contain at least one frame where a named character ACTS ON
another named character or is CHANGED by an environment: the fancier and the
rival adjusting aerials at each other; the soldier's hands and the bird; the
widower and the returning hen; the auctioneer and the paddles; the official
and the jar. Frames of a lone figure in a void are rationed to the sky act,
where loneliness IS the content.
